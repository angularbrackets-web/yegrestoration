# BK-46 — The appointment screen does not tell the truth about money or mail

**Status:** committed 2026-08-20 on `deploy-2-prepay` (`e04ba21`) — **DEPLOYED
2026-08-20**, fast-forward to `8854dd7`
**Phase:** P9 (post-deploy) · **Tier:** Reviewed — it is the screen the office
reads while a customer disputes a charge, and three of the four findings are
about money · **Deploy:** shipped 2026-08-20, ahead of Deploy 3.
**Migration:** none expected.

Found 2026-08-20 by reviewing `/admin/appointments/[id]` end to end against
screenshots of the first real end-to-end booking (#35), at the user's request,
after BK-44 established that the page's *controls* were ambiguous. These four
are about what the page *states*, and they were found the same way BK-44 and
BK-45 were: by a human reading the screen, not by a gate.

## The four

### 1. The header total excludes travel, structurally, and always

`[id].astro:87` builds the header's Assessment figure:

```ts
const quote = appointment && appointment.assessment_tier
  ? assessmentQuote({
      tier: appointment.assessment_tier,
      service: appointment.service,
      slotStart: appointment.slot_start,
    })
  : null;
```

`assessmentQuote` takes an optional `travelFeeCents` that **defaults to zero**
(`booking-pricing.ts:188-189`, *"a travel fee is only ever one an admin
typed"*). This call does not pass it. So the header's

> *"$606.38 total incl. GST"*

is base + GST with the travel fee omitted — on every row, at every status,
including rows where a travel fee was approved and charged.

**The user confirmed 2026-08-20 that travel is the figure the office adjusts
most often.** So on a routine out-of-radius booking the header understates what
the customer actually paid, and it does so on the one screen somebody reads
while that customer is on the phone about the amount.

### 2. The header recomputes from today's prices; the settled panel reads the snapshot

The same `quote` is recomputed from `(tier, service, slot_start)` rather than
read from the columns **migration 008** added. (This section originally
credited BK-32; plan review B3 established that 008 adds the four amount
columns and BK-23's approval screen writes them — `010-payments.ts` says so
itself. Corrected here because it is a live description; the quotation below
and the superseded plan further down are left verbatim as record.) The page's
own comment, written before those columns landed, called this out and was never
acted on:

> *"Recomputed from `(tier, service, slot_start)` rather than read off a column,
> because there is no amount column yet — BK-32 adds one, snapshotted at
> approval, **and at that point this display should read the snapshot instead**
> so the office sees what the customer was actually quoted rather than what the
> table says today."*

BK-32 shipped the column. The display was never switched. This is the same
shape as `update.ts:266` — a comment that was true when written, became false
when a dependency shipped, and was left standing as though it were still a
description. Finding 1 is the acute case of this one; the chronic case is that
a base-price change, a tier rename, or an override of the assessment figure all
put a different number in the header than in the panel below it.

**Both surfaces are labelled in dollars and neither says which one the customer
was charged.**

### 3. "Payment due by …" survives the payment

`markPaid()` writes `paid_at` and `payment_method` (`booking-payment.ts:530`,
`:610`) and never clears `payment_due_at`. The settled panel prints the due line
on the presence of `payment_due_at` alone, with no status test
(`[id].astro:974-978`). So a **paid** booking's only money panel reads:

> Amount settled at approval … **Payment due by Thu, Aug 20 · 11:39 a.m.**

The same stale line renders on `payment_expired`, `declined` and `cancelled`
rows, where it is equally untrue and arguably worse — it invites somebody to
chase a payment on a booking that is closed.

### 4. Nothing anywhere on this page says the booking was paid

`paid_at` and `payment_method` are written by `markPaid()` — which is the shared
path for **both** the Stripe webhook and the Interac button — and are **rendered
by nothing.** Grepped across `[id].astro` and `booking-admin.ts`: no reader.

What the office sees after a successful payment is a status badge reading
"Confirmed", a panel headed "Amount settled **at approval**", and finding 3's
stale due date. **They cannot see when it was paid, or whether the money came by
card or by e-Transfer.**

That is exactly the gap the settled panel was built to close, reappearing one
step later in the flow. Its own comment:

> *"Without this the office could approve an amount and then have no screen
> anywhere that showed what it was — `assessment_amount_cents` and its siblings
> were written by the approval and read by nothing. The figure a customer is
> disputing has to be visible to the person taking the call."*

Replace "approve" with "take payment" and "`assessment_amount_cents`" with
"`paid_at`" and the paragraph describes today.

It also matters operationally rather than only for the record: **card and
Interac are the two routes, and only one of them has a Stripe dashboard behind
it.** An office member asked to refund a booking cannot tell from this screen
whether to open Stripe or their bank.

## Why every gate missed all four

Same reason BK-44 and BK-45 were missed, and it is now the third instance:
**every verify script exercises a route, and none of them reads a screen.**
`verify-booking-payment.ts` proves `markPaid` writes `paid_at`. Nothing asks
whether anything displays it. A column written by a proven route and read by no
surface passes every test in the suite.

There is a second, sharper version here: **finding 1 is a defect the suite could
in principle catch** — the header total and the settled total are both derivable
and can be asserted equal for a row with a travel fee. That is a
*relationship* assertion between two surfaces, which is the class this repo has
now paid for three times. It is the pin worth building.

## Scope

- **The header reads the snapshot for any row past approval**, and falls back to
  the live recompute only for `pending_review` — where there is no snapshot and
  a suggestion is the honest thing to show. The two states should be
  distinguishable in the copy, not silently interchangeable.
- **Where a suggestion is shown, say so.** A `pending_review` header figure is
  an estimate; label it.
- **The header total includes travel** wherever travel exists, or the header
  stops claiming a total at all and defers to the settled panel.
- **The due line is conditional on the row still owing money** — not on
  `payment_due_at` being non-null.
- **The paid state is visible**: when it was paid, and by which route. Wording
  should distinguish "paid by card" from "marked paid by <actor>" — the second
  is a human claim about money and BK-32 deliberately recorded an author for it.
- **Fix the stale comment at `[id].astro:81-88`** rather than leaving it as a
  description of a past state.

## Risk areas for the reviewer

- **Do not let a snapshot-reading header break the `pending_review` case.** The
  approval screen's suggestion comes from the same recompute, and a row with no
  tier has neither. Three states, not two.
- **`assessment_amount_cents` is nullable.** A row past review with no snapshot
  must not render `$0.00` or crash. **Corrected at plan review (B3):** these are
  not old rows. `assessment_tier` is migration 007's and was never backfilled,
  so genuinely legacy rows carry no tier and answer `none`; what actually lands
  in that arm is a decline or a dropdown-cancel from this week.
- Finding 3's fix must not hide the due date from rows that genuinely still owe
  — it is the operational figure on `approved_awaiting_payment`.
- The GST figure is a stored column too; recomputing it in the header from a
  rate constant would reintroduce the same class of drift at 5%.

## Verification it owes

- **A relationship assertion, not four per-surface ones:** for a row with a
  non-zero travel fee, the header total and the settled total agree. This is the
  pin that would have caught finding 1, and its shape is the point.
- A red row per state — `pending_review` (suggestion), `approved_awaiting_payment`
  (settled + owing), `confirmed` (settled + paid), and a row past review with no
  snapshot.
- A red row proving the due line does not render on a paid row.
- A red row proving the paid route is distinguishable on screen.

### The shape of the fix: ONE derivation, not two agreeing ones

All four findings are the same defect wearing four hats — **the page derives
money in two places and the two disagree**. So the fix is not "make the header
include travel"; it is **delete the header's own derivation** and have both
surfaces read one function.

That has a consequence for the pin the ticket asks for, and it must be stated
rather than glossed. Once both read one function, *"the header total equals the
settled total"* is **true by construction and cannot fail** — the exact
tautology BK-45's plan review rejected (its B5). The honest decomposition is:

- a **behavioural** pin on the pure function: it includes travel, it reads the
  stored GST rather than recomputing at 5%, and it answers every row shape;
- a **source** pin that `[id].astro` has no second derivation — specifically
  that it does not call `assessmentQuote` for the header any more;
- and the ticket's relationship claim restated as what it actually becomes:
  **one source, asserted structurally.** Not dressed up as a comparison.

### T1 — `appointmentMoney(row)` in `booking-admin.ts`

A pure function returning a discriminated union, next to `notificationFlags`
and `customerStampState` — the module `verify-booking-admin.ts` already tests
without a database, which is what makes this assertable at all.

```ts
export type AppointmentMoney =
  | { kind: 'settled'; baseCents; travelCents; gstCents; totalCents }
  | { kind: 'estimate'; basis: 'suggestion' | 'no-snapshot';
      baseCents; totalCents; afterHours: boolean }
  | { kind: 'none'; reason: 'no-tier' };
```

**Three states, not two** (the reviewer's first risk area), and the third splits:

| Row | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `assessment_amount_cents !== null` | `settled` | The office settled it. Read all four columns, **including `gst_cents`** — never recompute at 5%, which is risk area 4 |
| `pending_review` + a tier | `estimate` / `suggestion` | No snapshot exists yet and a suggestion is the honest thing |
| past review, a tier, **no snapshot** | `estimate` / `no-snapshot` | A row approved before migration 010. It must render neither `$0.00` nor a claim that this is what was charged |
| no tier | `none` / `no-tier` | Today's "Not chosen — phone booking" |

`basis` exists so the COPY can differ where the numbers cannot: a
`pending_review` estimate is *"if you approve as suggested"*, and a pre-BK-32
estimate is *"today's price — this booking predates the amount snapshot"*.
Collapsing them would make a historical row look like a pending decision.

## Plan review (2026-08-20, fresh agent) — six blockers, all real

Every one was re-verified against the code before being acted on. The diagnosis
of the four findings was confirmed sound; **the plan was where it broke**, and
the two biggest misses are both cases of the plan asserting something about the
codebase it had not read.

### B1 — the structural pin was unimplementable · **FIXED**

The plan's P2 was *"`[id].astro` … does not call `assessmentQuote`"*. The file
calls it **twice**: `:96` is the header, and `:375` is `suggestedQuote`, the
approval form's pre-fill, consumed at `:1220-1276`. A file-wide ban is either
permanently red or deletes the office's pre-fill.

And even scoped to the header it was weak: `gstFor` is imported and legitimately
used at `:445`, so an inline
`formatCents(a.assessment_amount_cents! + a.travel_fee_cents + gstFor(...))`
would pass it green. **That is the answer to "what would this not catch": any
inline arithmetic over the four money columns.** The ROADMAP already records the
naive form of this — *"a source pin is satisfied by the import line"*.

Now anchored to the Assessment `<dd>` block with comments and imports stripped.

### B2 — `pending_review` can carry a snapshot, and the plan's table did not say which rule wins · **FIXED, and it fixes a second surface**

`update.ts:148-172`'s SET clause writes status, stage, notes, tier and
`cancelled_at` — **not the amount columns**. `editorMaySetStatus` permits
`approved_awaiting_payment -> pending_review`, so the dropdown walks a row back
and **leaves its snapshot behind**. `review.ts`'s own `rollBack` nulls those
columns and says why: *"leaving them set would make the admin page render
'Amount settled at approval' over a row nobody has approved."*

That is already true today on the dropdown path — **an existing defect this
ticket walked past**. So the answer covers both surfaces: on `pending_review`
the money is the SUGGESTION, and the settled panel does not render at all.

### B3 — the plan's own copy would have shipped a false claim · **FIXED**

`basis: 'no-snapshot'`, copy *"this booking predates the amount snapshot"*, and
the ticket's "pre-BK-32 row" are all wrong twice over:

- **The amount columns are migration 008's, not 010's.** `010-payments.ts:20`
  says so directly: they *"are **migration 008's**"*. `008:151-154` adds them.
- **The rows that actually reach that arm are not old at all.** `assessment_tier`
  is 007's and was never backfilled, so the thirteen legacy rows have a NULL
  tier and land in `no-tier`. What lands in this arm is a **`declined` or
  dropdown-`cancelled` row that was never approved** — an online booking carries
  a tier from the form and `decline` writes no amounts. A booking declined this
  morning would have been labelled as predating a migration applied yesterday.

**A new false sentence, introduced by the fix, on the screen this ticket exists
to make honest** — CLAUDE.md's grep-the-claim trap, self-inflicted, and the
third ticket running to hit it. The basis is now `never-settled` and its copy
claims nothing about age.

### B4 — the paid block would have rendered a previous cycle's payment · **FIXED (display half), write half DEFERRED IN WRITING**

The ROADMAP predicted this by name: *"It becomes visible the moment BK-46
renders those columns."* Nothing clears `paid_at` or `payment_method` — not

`confirmed` row → dropdown to `pending_review` → re-approve. The plan would have
rendered "Payment due by …" and "Paid by card · &lt;old timestamp&gt;" in one panel.

The paid block is now gated on `payment_status === 'paid'`. **The staleness
itself is NOT fixed here** — clearing those columns is a write-path change to
`approve`/`rollBack` on a live system, and it is separable. Deferred with an

### B5 — `stillOwesPayment` was wrong in two directions · **FIXED**

## Acceptance criteria (completed at plan review, 2026-08-20)

The reviewer's fifteen criteria are adopted verbatim as the ticket's own and are
reproduced here rather than summarised, because a criterion paraphrased is a
criterion weakened. See the plan-review record above for the reasoning behind
each; the numbering below is the reviewer's.

1. `appointmentMoney(row)` is a pure export of `booking-admin.ts`, reads only
   `Appointment` columns, and is exercised by `verify-booking-admin.ts` with no
   database, env or network.
2. `kind` and `basis` come from a compiler-exhaustive mapping, so a new state is
   a typecheck error rather than a fallthrough.
3. A red row per row-shape — (a) `pending_review` + tier + no snapshot;
   (b) `pending_review` + **snapshot present**; (c) `approved_awaiting_payment`
   + snapshot + unpaid; (d) `confirmed` + snapshot + paid; (e) `declined` /
   dropdown-`cancelled` with a tier and no snapshot, copy making **no** age
   claim; (f) legacy `confirmed`, no tier; (g) the `approveFree` $0 row;
   (h) `travel_fee_cents > 0`, where the header total equals
   `total_amount_cents` and exceeds base + `gstFor(base)`.
4. The `settled` arm derives nothing: a fixture with `gst_cents ≠ gstFor(...)`
   **and** `total_amount_cents ≠ base+travel+gst`, one red row per break, and a
   comment saying the fixture is deliberately un-producible by `review.ts`.
5. One header derivation, pinned structurally, anchored to the Assessment `<dd>`
   with comments and imports stripped: no money column named, no
   `assessmentQuote(`, no `gstFor(`, no arithmetic operator. Red-observed by
   reintroducing an inline `gstFor` total.
6. `suggestedQuote` and `confirming` untouched; the review form renders the same
   numbers; no pin forbids `assessmentQuote` file-wide.
7. The due line renders **iff** `approved_awaiting_payment` and unpaid and
   `total > 0` and `payment_due_at IS NOT NULL`; a pay-now row renders the
   no-deadline sentence; no `formatSlot` receives `Date | null`.
