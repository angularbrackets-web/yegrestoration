## Session creation, at approval

- `mode: 'payment'`, `currency: 'cad'`. **Line items come from the amount
  columns the approval screen wrote** (`assessment_amount_cents`,
  `travel_fee_cents`, `gst_cents`) — amended 2026-08-18, when the admin gained
  the ability to adjust them. They were pre-filled from
  `assessmentQuote(tier, service, slot_start)`, so the table is still where a
  number originates; the row is where it is fixed. **Never from anything in a
  request**, and never recomputed here — recomputing at session time would let a
  price change between approval and payment silently overwrite what the customer
  was quoted in the approval email.
- **Up to three line items**: the assessment (base), the travel fee **when it is
  non-zero**, and GST. A zero travel fee renders no line at all rather than a
  $0.00 one.
- **GST as its own line item** (client decision 2026-08-16), 5%, not folded
  into the tier price. The tier figures are ex-GST everywhere they render, and
  a single tax-inclusive line would silently contradict every surface.
- **The session total must equal `total_amount_cents` exactly.** Assert it
  before creating the session; a mismatch means the itemization and the charge
  disagree, and the email has already told the customer the itemization.
- `client_reference_id: String(appointment.id)`, `metadata: { appointment_id,
  tier }`.
- `success_url: <site>/book/confirmed/?session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}`,
  `cancel_url: <site>/book/payment-cancelled/`.
- `expires_at` from the payment deadline, clamped to Stripe's 30min–24h window;
  beyond 24h our own cron owns expiry.
- Stripe `Idempotency-Key: appointment-<id>-approval-<approved_at epoch>` — a
  double-click on Approve reuses the session instead of minting a second one.

**Payment deadline** = `min(approved_at + PAYMENT_WINDOW_HOURS, slot_start −
4h)`, `PAYMENT_WINDOW_HOURS = 12`. Stored on the row as `payment_due_at`, not
derived at read time: the constants may move under a live row, and a customer's
deadline must not move with them.

### The pay-now branch — required, not an optimisation

When `slot_start − now < PAY_NOW_THRESHOLD_HOURS` (propose 8) at approval, the
deferred deadline is **skipped entirely**: the link goes immediately,
`payment_due_at` is left NULL, and the expiry cron must not touch the row.

Without it, "payment always precedes dispatch" (client, 2026-08-16) destroys
exactly the jobs the client cares most about. A 2am emergency for 3am has
`slot_start − 4h` an hour in the past at the moment of approval, so the formula
yields a deadline that has already lapsed and the cron cancels the booking on
its next 15-minute pass — an emergency auto-cancelled while the crew is getting
in the van.

**Keyed on time-to-slot, not on an `is_emergency` flag.** Nobody has to
remember to tick anything, and a near-term *web* booking gets the same correct
treatment for free. A NULL `payment_due_at` is the signal to the cron, which
makes the exclusion a property of the data rather than a second predicate that
can drift.

What replaces the deadline is simply that the row is not `confirmed`: the crew
does not dispatch, and the office can see why on the admin page.

**`success_url` confirms nothing.** The webhook is the only thing that writes
`confirmed`. A customer who closes the tab mid-redirect is still confirmed; a
customer who hand-crafts the success URL is not.

## Webhook

`src/pages/api/stripe/webhook.ts`, `prerender = false`.

- Signature verified against `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` over the **raw body** —
  `await request.text()`, never `request.json()` first, which re-serializes and
  breaks the signature.
- Handles `checkout.session.completed`,
  `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.expired`,
  `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`. **Returns 200 to everything else** —
  a non-2xx makes Stripe retry for days.
- **Explicitly outside the admin middleware.** It is not under `/api/admin`, so
  it is already outside; the point is that it must stay outside and must never
  be "fixed" by adding auth. Stripe authenticates with a signature, not a
  cookie.

### Idempotency, three layers

Stripe redelivers by design, so one guard is not enough.

1. **`stripe_events (event_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY, type TEXT, received_at
   TIMESTAMPTZ)`.** First statement of the handler:
   `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING event_id`. Zero rows = already
   processed; return 200 and do nothing.
2. **The transition is a guarded update** — the idiom `update.ts` already uses:
   `UPDATE appointments SET status='confirmed', payment_status='paid',
   paid_at=$now WHERE id=$1 AND status='approved_awaiting_payment' RETURNING
   ...`. Zero rows = not our transition to make.
3. **Mail sends only when layer 2 returned a row**, under a transition-carrying
   idempotency prefix (BK-23 task 0).

### The confirm seam

The transition lives in **one** function —
`markPaid(appointmentId, { method, amountCents, ref })` — which the webhook
calls and which a Terminal handler could later call unchanged.

**On-site payment and card readers are out of scope** (client is sourcing
hardware separately). Two cheap things keep the door open and cost nothing now:
`payment_method` exists as a column from day one, and there is one confirm
function rather than a branch in the email layer and a second status path.

## Inherited from BK-23's implementation review (2026-08-18)

Three items handed here rather than fixed on BK-23, because BK-32 is what makes
each of them real.

1. **A re-approval at a corrected amount is silently swallowed.** The Resend
   prefix carries the transition but not the attempt, so a second
   `payment-link` send to one address inside the dedupe window is collapsed and
   reported as sent. On BK-23 this is latent — the guarded UPDATE already
   returns zero rows on a second approve, so there is no correction path to
   swallow. **BK-32 creates one**: "once a Checkout Session exists the amount is
   frozen; changing it means cancelling and re-approving" is exactly the
   sequence that re-sends a `payment-link` to the same customer. The fix is an
   attempt-varying component in the prefix, which `inviteIdempotencyPrefix(id,
   kind, now)` already does on the calendar side and the mail path has no
   equivalent of. **Prerequisite, not a nicety** — without it the corrected
   amount is stored, the flash says the customer was emailed, and the only
   email they hold quotes the wrong figure.

2. **A $0.00 total must not open a Checkout Session.** `parseAmountCents`
   accepts zero deliberately (a goodwill visit, a zero travel fee). Under
   prepay a $0 total is a booking that needs no payment step, not one with a
   broken step — decide the branch here rather than discovering it at Stripe.

3. **`reportBookingConversion` now fires on `/book/received/`** for a request
   that is not approved and not paid. When `success_url` lands on
   `/book/confirmed/`, the same sessionStorage payload may fire a second
   conversion for one booking. Decide which event is the conversion — the
   request or the payment — before wiring the success URL.

Also relevant: the **admin Resend button** collides with the booking-time
confirmation for the same reason as item 1, and is recorded in ROADMAP's Known
traps with owner BK-32.

## Interac e-Transfer — the manual path (added 2026-08-18)

New scope, merged in from the external spec. **No inbox parsing, no
auto-matching** — deliberately deferred, and saying so here is what stops the
next reader building it.

**`markPaid()` is the seam, and it is the whole design.**

```ts
markPaid(appointmentId, { method, amountCents, ref, actor? })
```

One function performs the `approved_awaiting_payment → confirmed` transition,
writes the payment columns, and fires the confirmation email with its `REQUEST`
ics. **Two callers, one code path:** the Stripe webhook, and the admin's
"Mark as paid — Interac" action. There is no second confirmation path, no
branch in the email layer, and no status logic anywhere but here. A Terminal
handler would later be a third caller and need nothing new.

- **Approval email offers both**: the Stripe link, and *"or send an Interac
  e-Transfer to `INTERAC_EMAIL` — include your booking number #`<id>` in the
  message"*. `INTERAC_EMAIL` is config, beside the other addresses in
  `booking-config.ts`.
- **Admin action** on `approved_awaiting_payment` rows only. Records
  `payment_method = 'interac'`, `interac_marked_by`, `interac_marked_at`, and
  the amount that was expected — the office is asserting money arrived, so who
  said so and when is the audit trail.
- **The race is real and it no-ops.** A customer pays by Interac, the office
  marks it, and the customer then pays the Stripe link too. The guarded update
  (`WHERE id = $1 AND status = 'approved_awaiting_payment'`) returns zero rows
  for the second arrival, so **no second confirmation email and no second ics**
  — and the webhook must then set `needs_attention` rather than silently
  discarding a real payment. **Do not auto-refund.** Flag it; a human issues the
  refund in the Stripe dashboard.
- The same guard makes the reverse order safe: Stripe first, Interac mark
  second, the admin action reports "already confirmed" instead of double-firing.

## Expiry cron — **BUILT 2026-08-19**, ahead of the rest of this ticket

`/api/cron/expire-payments/`, in `vercel.json`, every 15 minutes. The handler,
both sweeps, the auth and the apology message exist and are gated; what remains
of BK-32 is Stripe itself.

**Built early deliberately.** Sweep 2 is BK-23 Task 4, which became a Deploy 2
blocker, and it had nowhere to live but this handler. Sweep 1 needs no Stripe
column — `payment_due_at` and `payment_status` are migration 008's — so
building it alongside cost nothing and avoids a second pass over the same file.
What sweep 1 does NOT do yet is anything Stripe-side: **BK-32 must cancel the
Checkout Session when it expires a row**, or a customer can pay a link for a
booking whose slot has already been released. That is the race this ticket's own
section below is about, and it is not handled yet.

**Two sweeps in one handler**, added 2026-08-19. This ticket owns the handler,
the schedule and the payment sweep; **BK-23 Task 4 owns the second sweep's rule
and its message** and was moved into Deploy 2 for it. One handler rather than
two crons: both are "transitions that fall due with nobody watching", on the
same table, on the same interval, and two schedules is two things to reason
about at 3am for no gain.

| Sweep | Selects | Becomes | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment | `approved_awaiting_payment AND payment_due_at < now()` | `payment_expired` | BK-32 |
| Stale request | `pending_review AND slot_start < now() + interval '4 hours'` | `declined` | **BK-23 Task 4** |

The sweeps must not be collapsed into one statement. They select different
statuses on different columns and produce different terminal states and
different customer emails; the only thing they share is when they run.

**Sweep 1 — payment.** Slot released, polite customer email with the call-us
line and an invitation to rebook, office notified. Same guarded-update idiom.

**Sweep 2 — stale requests.** A `pending_review` row holds its slot by design,
and nothing else ever releases it: this sweep is the only automatic exit, and
BK-23's S3 guard now refuses to approve a slot that has already passed, so
without it a lapsed request is stuck in a slot-holding status with its only
transition out blocked. `declined` rather than `payment_expired` — no payment
was ever requested, so the latter would be false and would corrupt the payment
columns. **Its message is not the at-capacity decline**: that copy is a claim
about our schedule, and this customer was simply missed. Full rule, reasoning
and the verification it owes are on BK-23 Task 4.

**A NULL `payment_due_at` is not eligible for sweep 1** (the pay-now branch),
and a row in `pending_review` is not eligible for it either — it has no
`approved_at`, no amount and no deadline. Sweep 2 is the only one that may
touch it.

**The race is real and it has a wrong answer.** The webhook's guard names
`approved_awaiting_payment`, so if the cron wins by a second the payment lands
on an already-expired row. Do **not** auto-refund silently — mark
`payment_status = 'paid'`, flag the row for attention, and alert the office. A
customer who paid and got auto-refunded without a word is a worse outcome than
a slot the office has to sort out by phone.

### THE WEBHOOK URL — THE HOST *AND* THE TRAILING SLASH

```
https://www.yegrestoration.ca/api/stripe/webhook/
```

**`www.`, and the final slash. Both. Either one wrong and every payment event
fails, silently, in production only.**

**The host was wrong in this ticket until 2026-08-19**, when it was curled
during the rollout rather than read. This file said
`https://yegrestoration.ca/…` — the apex — and the apex **307-redirects to
`www`**, on the API path too:

```
$ curl -X POST -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code} -> %{redirect_url}" \
    https://yegrestoration.ca/api/stripe/webhook/
307 -> https://www.yegrestoration.ca/api/stripe/webhook/
```

Which is fatal for the same reason the slash is, and this ticket already knew
the reason — it just applied it to one of the two hops. Stripe's support
documentation: *"For webhooks, Stripe considers any redirect response to be a
failure,"* with the instruction to *"change the URL to the final URL, after
redirects, where Stripe Events should be delivered."* `www` is that final URL.
Verified as a real response rather than a redirect at that host.

**This is the BK-34a trap wearing a different hat** — a URL that looks correct,
resolves for a human, and does not resolve for the machine that matters. It was
found the only way this class ever is: by issuing the real request against the
real host. Nothing in `dist/`, in the generated route table, or in
`verify-stripe-webhook.ts` can see it, because all three reason about the PATH
and this is the HOST.

**Registered without the final slash, every payment event fails.**
`astro.config.mjs` sets `trailingSlash: 'always'`, so Vercel answers the
unslashed form with a **308** — and **Stripe does not follow redirects**. A
browser would; Stripe will not. The symptom is silent and production-only:
customers pay, Stripe records the payment, the endpoint never runs, and the
expiry cron releases their slot within twelve hours.

`scripts/verify-stripe-webhook.ts` asserts this against the **generated**
`.vercel/output/config.json` — that the slashed path reaches `_render` with zero
redirects, and, as its control, that the unslashed path really does 308. The URL
above is built from the same constant the route resolves to, so this note and
the route cannot drift.

### P0 — the ordering question, settled: **transition first, then Stripe**

The ticket asks for `Idempotency-Key: appointment-<id>-approval-<approved_at
epoch>`, and today `createCheckoutUrl()` is awaited at `review.ts:274`,
**before** the guarded UPDATE at `:295` stamps `approved_at`. So at
session-creation time there is no stored epoch, and a double-click carries two
different `now()`s — two keys, two Sessions. The key as specified cannot be
computed where the call currently sits.

**The order inverts.** The guarded UPDATE runs first and returns what it wrote:

```sql
UPDATE appointments
   SET status = 'approved_awaiting_payment', payment_status = 'pending',
       approved_at = $now, assessment_amount_cents = $base, ...
 WHERE id = $1 AND status = 'pending_review'
RETURNING id, approved_at, assessment_amount_cents, travel_fee_cents,
          gst_cents, total_amount_cents, payment_due_at, stripe_session_id
```

Three things fall out:

1. **The idempotency key becomes computable** — `approved_at` is a stored fact
   by the time the Session is created.
2. **An orphan *approval* becomes structurally impossible.** A second click's
   UPDATE returns zero rows and never reaches Stripe at all. **An orphan
   *session* remains possible** in the narrow window named in P0.5 — the first
   draft of this plan claimed otherwise, conflating the two, and the review was
   right to call it. The key still earns its keep against a network-level retry
   of one POST inside one approval, which is what a Stripe idempotency key is
   actually for.
3. **"Built from the STORED amount columns" stops being a claim about
   variables that happen to match and becomes literally true.** The line items
   are built from the `RETURNING` row.

**What this costs.** BK-23's route builds the message before the UPDATE so it
can *refuse to transition* when the customer cannot be told (`review.ts:51-57`).
Under the new order the card link is not known until after. The refusal splits
into the part answerable without a network and the part that is not:

| Checked | When | On failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the row has an email address | **before** the UPDATE (unchanged) | `review=noemail`, row stays `pending_review` |
| *some* payment method is configured — `INTERAC_EMAIL !== null \|\| stripeConfigured()` | **before** the UPDATE | `review=nopayment`, row stays `pending_review` |
| the Session was actually created | after the UPDATE | Interac configured → degrade to Interac-only. Interac **not** configured → **roll the transition back** (below) |

The pre-UPDATE email check is a byte-faithful substitute for building the
message: `approvalMessage` returns null on exactly one condition,
`if (!input.email) return null` (`booking-email.ts:508`). `canOfferPayment`
(`:722`) is a pure two-field check over configuration.

**The rollback, and why it replaces the flag the first draft proposed.** When
the Session cannot be created and there is no Interac address, no session
exists — so nothing can pay and nothing can race. The transition is undone
rather than flagged:

```sql
UPDATE appointments
   SET status = 'pending_review', payment_status = 'not_required',
       approved_at = NULL, assessment_amount_cents = NULL, travel_fee_cents = 0,
       gst_cents = NULL, total_amount_cents = NULL, payment_due_at = NULL,
       updated_at = $now
 WHERE id = $1 AND status = 'approved_awaiting_payment'
   AND stripe_session_id IS NULL
```

That preserves `review.ts:51-57`'s rule exactly instead of downgrading it to a
flag. **The amount columns must be nulled too**, or `[id].astro`'s `settled`
panel (`:340`) renders "Amount settled at approval" over a `pending_review` row.
Unreachable in today's configuration — `INTERAC_EMAIL` is a non-null constant
(`booking-config.ts:384`) — and written anyway, because "unreachable today" is a
property of a constant somebody may change.

### P0.5 — who writes `stripe_session_id`, and the one window that stays open

The first draft never named this write, and two acceptance criteria depend on
it. Sequence, in full:

1. guarded UPDATE → transition, `RETURNING` (including the *previous*
   `stripe_session_id`)
2. **expire the previous Session first, and treat a failure as fatal** — roll
   back per P0 and report `review=stale-session`. Proceeding would violate the
   "one live link" rule; the alternative of accepting the window would leave the
   old link payable at the old price for as long as the mint takes.
3. create the new Session, under `withDeadline` (P1)
4. **record it:**
   `UPDATE appointments SET stripe_session_id = $2 WHERE id = $1 AND status = 'approved_awaiting_payment' AND stripe_session_id IS DISTINCT FROM $2`
5. build and send the approval email

**The window that stays open:** a crash, a timeout or a failed step 4 leaves a
live Checkout Session no row knows about. It cannot be closed without a
distributed transaction, so it is *named* instead: step 4's failure is logged
and sets `needs_attention`, and the webhook's amount check (P1) is what stops
such a session confirming a booking at a stale price. This is the honest
version of the claim the first draft overstated.

### P1 — the seam widens (`src/lib/booking-payment.ts`)

The module header's claim that "the caller does not change" predates the
2026-08-18 itemization amendment and is **deleted**; the ticket wins over the
comment.

```ts
export type CheckoutRequest = {
  appointmentId: number;
  tier: AssessmentTier;          // metadata
  baseCents: number;             // stored assessment_amount_cents
  travelCents: number;           // stored travel_fee_cents
  gstCents: number;              // stored gst_cents
  totalCents: number;            // stored total_amount_cents
  dueAt: Date | null;            // stored payment_due_at; null = pay-now
  slotStart: Date;               // the pay-now expires_at clamp reads it
  approvedAt: Date;              // the stored epoch, for the idempotency key
  customerEmail: string;         // Stripe's own receipt, and dashboard search
  now: Date;                     // NOT approvedAt — see below
  previousSessionId: string | null;
};

export type CheckoutSession = { url: string; sessionId: string };

export async function createCheckoutSession(
  request: CheckoutRequest,
  deps?: StripeDeps,
): Promise<CheckoutSession | null>;

export async function expireCheckoutSession(
  sessionId: string,
  deps?: StripeDeps,
): Promise<boolean>;
```

**`now` is a required field and is not `approvedAt`.** Stripe's 30-minute
`expires_at` floor is measured from *creation*, not from the transition, and
`booking-review.ts:4` forbids this family of module reading its own clock
("no clock of its own. Everything takes an explicit `now`, so a verify script
can drive the boundary cases"). Without it `checkoutExpiresAt` would be fully
asserted while the caller fed it something else.

`null` keeps its current meaning — no card processor configured.
`createCheckoutUrl` is renamed rather than wrapped: a function returning a URL
when the caller needs a URL *and* an id will be called wrongly once.

**The amount assertions, and why the obvious one is worthless.** Asserting
`base + travel + gst === total` on the values from one `RETURNING` row is a
tautology: one statement wrote all four from `subtotalCents + gstCents`
(`review.ts:257-260`), so both sides move together. It is the eighth shape of
the family this repo has now found seven times, and it is **not** what defends
the ticket's named risk. The two that can actually fail:

- **after `sessions.create`: `session.amount_total === totalCents`.** Stripe
  echoes what it will really charge. A mismatch expires the session and refuses
  the approval.
- **in the webhook: `event.data.object.amount_total === row.total_amount_cents`.**
  The only place a stale link from a previous approval at a different price
  becomes visible — including P0.5's untracked session. A mismatch confirms
  nothing, flags `needs_attention`, and alerts the office.

The by-construction identity is still asserted, once, in the pure layer where
it *can* fail (over independently supplied numbers), and is labelled as pinning
arithmetic rather than integrity.

**Session shape.** `mode: 'payment'`, `currency: 'cad'`; `price_data` line items
with `unit_amount` in cents and `quantity: 1` — Assessment, Travel (**only when
non-zero**), GST; `client_reference_id: String(id)`; `metadata: { appointment_id,
tier }`; `customer_email`; `success_url` / `cancel_url` built from
`booking-config.ts`, never spelled at a call site.

**Absolute URLs.** `success_url` and `cancel_url` must be absolute, and no
site-origin constant exists — every absolute URL in this codebase is built at
the page from `Astro.site` (`[id].astro:150`). The route passes `context.site`
into the builders, and the route-table script asserts the built success URL is
absolute *and* slashed, not just that the webhook path resolves.

**`expires_at`**, via `checkoutExpiresAt(dueAt, slotStart, now)` — living in
`booking-payment.ts`, not `booking-review.ts`, because it is a Stripe fact and
that module is BK-23's pure review arithmetic:

- deferred: `clamp(dueAt, now+30min, now+24h)`
- pay-now (`dueAt === null`): `clamp(slotStart, now+30min, now+24h)`

With `PAY_NOW_THRESHOLD_HOURS = 8` and `PAYMENT_DEADLINE_LEAD_HOURS = 4`, a
deferred `dueAt` is bounded to `[now+4h, now+12h]` (`booking-review.ts:40-57`),
so **neither clamp bound is ever reached on the deferred branch and
`expires_at === dueAt` exactly**. Asserted as that relationship, not as an
observation, so moving a constant cannot silently create a link that dies before
its own stated deadline.

**Two pay-now consequences, stated rather than left to be rediscovered:** when
the slot is under 30 minutes away the floor pushes `expires_at` past
`slot_start`, so the link stays payable after the visit has started; and a NULL
`payment_due_at` means nothing ever expires that row, so an unpaid pay-now
booking holds its slot indefinitely. Both are consistent with P9's "the office
chases it by phone" and neither is a defect to fix here.

**Lazy client.** `new Stripe(key)` is constructed **inside** the function, after
the key check — `booking-notify.ts:19-22` records the same lesson for
`new Resend(key)`. A module-scope client would throw at import time and break
every verify script that transitively imports `review.ts`.

**Injectable.** `StripeDeps` mirrors `NotifyDeps` (`booking-notify.ts:46-64`):
the suite drives success, an API error and a hang with no key and no network.

**Deadlined.** The create runs under
`withDeadline(..., CHECKOUT_BUDGET_MS, null)` and the null enters the
degradation branch. A **new, smaller constant, not `POST_COMMIT_BUDGET_MS`**:
that budget is 5000ms and already covers the mail send on this same path
(`review.ts:317-321`), and the config comment at `booking-config.ts:427`
specifically warns that stacking two 5s races blows the platform's 10s default
and returns a 504 for work that committed. `CHECKOUT_BUDGET_MS = 3000`, and the
arithmetic is written down beside it.

### P2 — `markPaid()`, the one confirmation path

```ts
markPaid(sql, appointmentId, {
  method: 'stripe' | 'interac' | 'onsite' | 'none',
  amountCents: number,     // what actually arrived
  reference: string | null,// payment_intent, or the office's e-Transfer ref
  sessionId?: string,      // stripe only
  actor?: string,          // the admin who asserted it; Stripe has none
  now: Date,
}): Promise<MarkPaidOutcome>
```

Guarded UPDATE, `WHERE id = $1 AND status = 'approved_awaiting_payment'`,
`RETURNING *`. One row → write the payment columns, then send.

**Where the money goes, and where it does not.** `amountCents` is written to
**`paid_amount_cents`** and `reference` to **`payment_reference`** — two columns
migration 010 adds for them (B2). `total_amount_cents` is **never written after
approval**: it is the approval-time snapshot, and overwriting it with what
Stripe reports would destroy the one invariant the ticket states twice.
`stripe_payment_intent_id` is written **only** when `method === 'stripe'`, or an
e-Transfer reference lands in the column BK-33 reads to issue a refund.

Zero rows → branch on what the row actually is, **and on whether this is the
same payment arriving again**:

| Row is | Same reference? | Meaning | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `confirmed` | **yes** | one payment, delivered twice | outcome `'already-recorded'`. **No flag, no alert**, 200 |
| `confirmed` | no | **double pay** | append `needs_attention`, alert the office, **no second email, no second ics, no refund** |
| `payment_expired` / `declined` / `cancelled` | — | **paid after release** | narrow UPDATE writes the payment columns and `needs_attention` **without touching `status`**. Alert the office |
| anything else | — | not our transition | log, outcome `'not-applicable'` |

**The same-reference row is a blocker fix, not a refinement.** Without it every
retry after a partial failure, and every session that emits both
`checkout.session.completed` and `async_payment_succeeded`, tells the office
"double payment — issue a refund" for a single charge. The branch whose whole
job is to be trustworthy about money would be the one that cries wolf, and the
ticket's instruction is that a human acts on that alert.

**The narrow UPDATE restates its expectations** —
`AND status IN ('payment_expired','declined','cancelled') AND payment_status <> 'paid'`
— because `review.ts:46` states the house rule categorically: "There is no
SELECT-then-UPDATE anywhere in here." Without it a concurrent Interac mark can
have its method, stamp and reference overwritten by the losing side.

**Never auto-refund**, on any branch.

**Layer 3 is structural.** The mail sits inside the `updated.length === 1`
branch and is unreachable from every zero-row branch. What it sends is what the
inward invite crossing already sends — `planFirstConfirmationEmail` to the
customer and `planCalendarInvite` to the office (`booking-admin-notify.ts:407`,
`:256`), both already used by `update.ts:sendBoundaryMail`. **Reused, not
re-implemented**: a second confirmation builder is exactly the "branch in the
email layer and a second status path" the confirm seam forbids. It also
inherits BK-23's B2 fix, so a payment-confirmed row is never told it was
"reinstated".

`needs_attention` is **appended**, never overwritten — BK-40's `admin_notes`
rule for a second column. **Nothing clears it, and that is deliberate for this
ticket**: the office reads it on the list and detail pages, and an
acknowledge-and-clear action is out of scope, recorded here so its absence is
not read as an oversight.

**Two callers:** `api/stripe/webhook.ts` and
`api/admin/appointments/mark-paid.ts`. Checked against the ROADMAP's
sibling-route trap: there is no dynamic route under `api/admin/appointments/`
(create, file-delete, resend, review, update only), so the shape is safe, and
the route-table script pins it.

### P3 — the webhook (`src/pages/api/stripe/webhook.ts`)

`prerender = false`, `POST`. **`await request.text()` is the first thing that
touches the body** — `request.json()` re-serializes and breaks the signature.
Verified against `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`; a bad signature is **400**, because
Stripe retries 5xx and a forgery is not something to retry.

**Layer 1 is a claim, not a sighting.** The first draft made it
`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` and read zero rows as "already processed" —
which treats *recorded* as *processed*, with no state between. A handler that
dies after the insert and before `markPaid` commits (a function timeout, an OOM,
a deploy rollover) makes Stripe's retry a no-op: the row stays
`approved_awaiting_payment`, `expire-payments.ts:107-129` flips it to
`payment_expired` within the deadline, the slot is released and the customer is
emailed an apology. **Money taken, booking cancelled, nothing flagged.**

So `stripe_events` carries `processed_at TIMESTAMPTZ`, and the claim is:

```sql
INSERT INTO stripe_events (event_id, type, received_at)
VALUES ($1, $2, $3)
ON CONFLICT (event_id) DO UPDATE SET event_id = EXCLUDED.event_id
  WHERE stripe_events.processed_at IS NULL
RETURNING event_id
```

Zero rows means **processed**, not seen. `processed_at` is stamped after
`markPaid` returns. Re-running `markPaid` on a retry is already safe — its
guarded UPDATE is the real dedupe — *provided* P2's same-reference branch
exists, or the retry lands in the false double-pay branch instead.

| Event | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `checkout.session.completed`, `payment_status === 'paid'` | amount check, then `markPaid(..., 'stripe')` |
| `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` | same |
| `checkout.session.completed`, `payment_status === 'unpaid'` | nothing — an async method is still pending |
| `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` | `payment_status = 'failed'` + `needs_attention`; **status untouched** |
| `checkout.session.expired` | recorded; **status untouched** — the cron owns row expiry |
| anything else | **200**, no-op |

**Explicitly outside the admin middleware, and it stays outside.**
`src/middleware.ts:6-11` matches `/admin` and `/api/admin` by prefix, so
`/api/stripe/webhook/` never enters it. Stripe authenticates with a signature,
not a cookie. A source-level assertion pins that those two prefixes do not cover
this path, so "fixing" it reads as a failure rather than a tidy-up.

The appointment id comes from `client_reference_id` / `metadata` and is parsed
as strictly as the admin detail route parses its own — a non-integer is a 200
with a logged error, never a crash.

### P4 — the cron's missing half

### P5 — the $0.00 branch

A zero total opens no Session. The approve path transitions to
`approved_awaiting_payment` as normal (stamping the zero amounts, so the screen
still shows what was settled) and **immediately calls `markPaid(id, { method:
'none', amountCents: 0 })`** in the same request.

Two guarded UPDATEs rather than a direct `pending_review → confirmed`, because
`markPaid`'s guard names `approved_awaiting_payment` and **it stays the one

`payment_status = 'paid'`, `payment_method = 'none'`, **not** `'not_required'`:

**The $0 branch is tested before the `noemail` and `nopayment` pre-checks**,
which it needs neither of — a goodwill visit with no email address is still a
booking the office should be able to confirm.

### P6 — the Resend prefix gains an attempt (inherited item 1)

The four source-level pins at `verify-booking-email.ts:893-921` survive

### P7 — the conversion (inherited item 3, N5)

**The conversion is the payment, not the request.** Decided; the rest is
mechanism.

- `BookingForm.svelte:556` — the call and its import are **deleted**. That
  branch is the sessionStorage-write-failed fallback on the *request* path.
- `BookingConfirmation.svelte:67` — fires only for `variant === 'confirmed'`.
- **Keyed on the Stripe `session_id` in the URL, not on the booking id** (user
  decision, 2026-08-19). `sessionStorage` is empty whenever the customer pays
  from the emailed link in another tab, browser or device — the common case, not
  the edge one — and keying on the booking id would drop the conversion for
  exactly those payers. The `session_id` is present on every legitimate
  post-payment landing, unique per payment, and stable across whether
  `sessionStorage` survived, so one payment yields one `transaction_id` either
  way. It is both the `transaction_id` and the dedupe marker.
- **The `session_id` is shape-validated** (`cs_live_` / `cs_test_` prefix, an
  expected length and alphabet) before it is treated as a landing at all.
  Nothing validates it against Stripe — the page makes no network call — so the
  shape check is a bar, not a proof, and is described as one.
- **`/book/confirmed/` requires a shape-valid `session_id`.** Without one it
  redirects to `/book/`, whatever `sessionStorage` holds.

**The panel is receipt-shaped unconditionally, and the stored payload is not
rendered on this page at all.** The first draft rendered the full slot/address/
reference panel when a payload happened to be present. The payload was written
by `/book/received/` for a *request* and has no relationship to the payment: a
customer who submits two requests in one tab and then pays for the first is
shown the second one's slot, address and reference under "You're booked". That
is the class of false claim P9 exists to remove, and it is the same argument the
user made for the no-payload copy — the page writes nothing and verifies
nothing, so it must not assert what only the webhook knows.

The copy is a receipt, not a state claim (user, 2026-08-19): *"Thanks — payment
received. Your confirmation email and calendar invite are on their way."* plus
the phone number. The details reach the customer in the confirmation email,
which `markPaid` sends and which is the artifact that actually knows they are
booked. **This narrows what a paying customer sees compared with today and is
flagged for the user as overturnable.**

The GA4 call omits `booking_id` — `conversionCalls` already omits it for a
non-positive id (`booking-confirmation.ts:130-135`), which is the right default
and is now stated rather than inherited.

**Accepted consequence, not to be fixed:** a customer who pays and closes the
tab never lands here, so their conversion never registers. **No server-side
conversion path.**

The decision logic is pure, in `booking-confirmation.ts`, so
`verify:booking:confirmation` asserts it without a browser.

### P9 — the webhook URL and the route table

**The trap:** under `trailingSlash: 'always'` a webhook registered without the
trailing slash receives a 308, and **Stripe does not follow redirects** — every
event fails, silently, in production only. A webhook is unsmokable in
`astro dev` by definition.

`scripts/verify-stripe-webhook.ts` (`npm run verify:stripe:webhook`, building
first) simulates the generated route table as
`verify-appointment-upload.ts:393-470` does:

- `/api/stripe/webhook/` survives the pre-filesystem phase **with no redirect**
  and reaches `_render`.
- the **unslashed** form **does** 308 — asserted positively. **This is the
  control**: it cannot pass alongside the assertion above it if the simulation
  is inert.
- no dot in any path segment, so the dot-stripping 308 cannot apply.
- the slashed URL the rollout note gives the user is built from the same
  constant the route resolves to, and the built `success_url` is asserted
  absolute and slashed.

### Acceptance criteria

1. **Approving stamps the transition first and mints the Session from what it
   wrote** — line items and total from the columns the guarded UPDATE returned,
   never recomputed, never from a request. **Stripe's own `amount_total` is
   checked against `total_amount_cents`** after creation, and again in the
   webhook before anything is confirmed.
2. **A double-click approves once.** The second attempt returns zero rows and
   never reaches Stripe. The idempotency key is
   `appointment-<id>-approval-<stored approved_at epoch>`.
3. **A re-approval expires the previous Session before minting**, and a failure
   to expire it aborts the approval rather than leaving two live links.
4. **A $0.00 total opens no Session** and reaches `confirmed` through
   `markPaid` — `payment_method = 'none'`, no payment-link email, and no email
   address required.
5. **The webhook verifies over the raw body**, is outside the admin middleware
   and stays outside, and returns **200 to every event it does not handle**.
6. **Three layers, and layer 1 records processing rather than receipt.** A
   handler that dies mid-flight does not swallow the payment: the retry is
   claimed and completes. Mail is unreachable without layer 2.
7. **`markPaid()` is the only path to `confirmed` for a payment,** with two
   callers. One payment arriving twice is a silent no-op; **two** payments flag,
   alert and are never refunded automatically; a payment landing on a released
   row records the money, flags it, and leaves `status` alone.
8. **The expiry cron cancels the Stripe Session of every row it expires,** and a
   NULL `payment_due_at` is still never expired — by predicate shape, no
   `IS NOT NULL`, no `COALESCE`.
9. **The notification idempotency prefix carries an attempt,** so a re-issued
   payment link and a second Resend both deliver — and the distinctness arms
   pin a frozen `now` so dropping the *type* still goes red.
10. **`reportBookingConversion` fires exactly once per payment, on
    `/book/confirmed/`, keyed on a shape-valid Stripe session id.**
    `/book/received/` fires nothing; a refresh, a Back, or a leftover request
    payload fire nothing, and no stored payload is rendered on that page.
11. **Migration 010 is additive only** and may run before the deploy.
12. **The slashed webhook URL resolves without a redirect** on the generated
    route table, proved by simulation with a control that must fail, and
    recorded as the URL for the Stripe dashboard.
13. Gates green: `typecheck`, `build`, all 22 existing verify scripts plus
    `verify:stripe:webhook`; every red row above observed.

## Implementation review (2026-08-19, fresh agent)

Reviewer was a fresh agent given the two diffs (`851ed16` and Task 4's

cron shipped in an earlier session and had never been independently reviewed.

**2 blockers, 7 should-fix, 7 nits. All accepted; none refused.**

### B1 — FIXED. `rollBack` could never run on the one path that reached it

`rollBack` is guarded `AND stripe_session_id IS NULL`, and its only reachable
caller sat inside `if (settled.stripe_session_id)`. Nothing ever wrote that
column back to NULL, so the guard was **false by construction** and the UPDATE
matched zero rows.

The consequence is the flow BK-23's approval screen documents: the office puts
an expired booking back to `pending_review` and re-approves at a corrected
amount. Stripe refuses to expire a session it has already closed — the ordinary
case, not an edge (see S1) — so the route aborted, "rolled back" nothing, and
returned `review=stale-session`. **The row was left approved, with a deadline
running, no email of any kind sent, and the office told the approval was
refused.** Twelve hours later the cron expired it and apologised to a customer
who had never been told they were approved.

Fixed by making the re-approval **continue rather than abort**. Aborting was
wrong in every direction: the transition has already committed, and a rollback
would leave a possibly-live link pointing at a `pending_review` row — where
`markPaid` refuses, so a payment against it is money recorded nowhere. What
actually protects the customer is the webhook's amount check: a payment on the
stale link either matches the stored total, in which case confirming is right,
or does not, and is refused and flagged. So the session is flagged by id, the
new link is minted, and the customer is emailed. The dead-session column is
also NULLed on a successful expire, which is what makes `rollBack`'s guard
effective on the paths that still use it.

### B2 — FIXED. A failed transition stamped the event anyway

`confirm()` discarded `markPaid`'s return. `markPaid` never throws — that is its
contract — so a failed confirm UPDATE came back as the **value** `'error'`, the
handler returned normally, `processed_at` was stamped, and Stripe got a 200 and
never retried.

That is the exact "money taken, booking cancelled, nothing flagged" outcome
`processed_at` was introduced to prevent, reopened one layer up — and on the
**more likely** failure rather than the more dramatic one: not a process death,
but a database error, which is precisely what a retry exists for. The
asymmetry that hid it: the `SELECT` in the same function *did* propagate and
give a 500. Only the write was swallowed.

Fixed by throwing on `'error'` so the event is left unstamped. `'missing'` is
deliberately **not** retried — the appointment does not exist and no amount of
retrying changes that, while a 500 would put Stripe into a multi-day loop over
an unusable event — so it is logged at error level and stamped.

**S1 — `expireCheckoutSession` could not tell "already closed" from "still
live".** Stripe errors on expiring any session that is not `open`, and on the
deferred branch `expires_at` equals `payment_due_at` exactly — so by the time
the sweep sees an overdue row Stripe has already closed its session. Collapsing
