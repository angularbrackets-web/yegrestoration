## It ships in the same deploy as the flip

The new terms describe a payment link. They must not reach the browser before
the link exists, and the current terms ("paid at the end of the visit") must
not survive the flip by even one deploy. Same release as BK-23 + BK-31 + BK-32,

## Inherited — BK-31's refused blocker B3, and it is why this is a release gate

**Recorded here on 2026-08-19.** It was in the ROADMAP's build order and in
BK-31's review, and nowhere on this ticket — so a reader who opened BK-36 alone,
which is exactly what the next session will do, would have missed the single
biggest thing it owes.

**`FEE_TERMS_OUTRO[0]` still reads *"Nothing is charged when you book. Tell the
tech on the day which of these you want."*** It renders three lines above the
binding radio group, and in the customer confirmation email immediately above
the `You chose: …` line. Under prepay both halves of that sentence are false:
something *is* charged when you book, and the choice does *not* wait for the day.

BK-31's reviewer raised it as a blocker. It was **refused there in writing**, on
the grounds that the replacement is client-facing copy and this ticket owns it —
writing it inside a BK-31 review fix would have been inventing customer-facing
terms without the client. The severity was accepted in full.

**The concrete case, and it is why "copy ticket" understates this.** Saturday,
service `mold`, tier `sketch`: the customer keeps an email whose terms paragraph
says the choice is made on the day and whose list says $1,199, immediately
followed by *"$1,777.50 + GST ($1,866.38 total) — weekend rate, 1.5x"*. A
written record contradicting itself about whether the choice binds **and** about
the amount, in a flow whose entire purpose is taking money up front.

**Also folded in from BK-31's nits:** `FEE_TERMS_INTRO`'s *"starting at $399 +
GST"* is a fourth place a price literal lives, is not covered by the copy pin,
and is false for mould and for every weekend slot. Same sentence family, same
owner.

**Deploy 2 must not ship without this ticket.** That is recorded in P9's build
order as a release gate rather than as a ticket dependency, and it is the reason
the other four tickets are finished and nothing has deployed.

## The four constraints, in order of how easily each is broken

1. **The word "deductible" appears nowhere.** Deductible rebating by
   contractors is explicitly illegal in several US jurisdictions and reads as
   claims-fraud territory to Canadian insurers. The credit is against *our
   invoice*; the copy never names what the customer's share is called.
2. **Nothing states or implies the assessment is billed to the insurer.** The
   $699/$1,199 language describes documentation the customer receives and can
   hand to their adjuster. It never says who pays.
3. **The block ends on the credit.** Refund and forfeiture language sits in the
   middle. The last thing read is the good news. This is why `FEE_TERMS_OUTRO`
   is split rather than edited — its second line currently ends the block on
   non-refundability.
4. **$699/$1,199 are reframed around the report**, because on an insurance job
   the deliverable is the customer's benefit, not the credit.

## Constants

`FEE_TERMS_OUTRO` splits into `FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT`, `FEE_TERMS_REFUND` and
`FEE_TERMS_CREDIT`. Full drafted text is in
`docs/booking/prepay-flow-spec.md` §5 and is the approved starting point
(client, 2026-08-16 — structure approved, wording still editable under the
standing "implementer drafts, client edits" rule).

Render order, on every surface, without exception:

```
HEADING → INTRO → ITEMS → PAYMENT → REFUND → CREDIT → [tier radios] → [ack checkbox]
```

The radios and the checkbox come last because the ack label says "the terms
above" — an ordering claim about the page, which `verify-cutover.ts` should pin
the way it already pins the terms-above-checkbox relationship.

**The `[PLACEHOLDER]` refund markers are FILLED — client answered 2026-08-18.**
`FEE_TERMS_REFUND` ships with real values, not "call us":

```ts
export const FEE_TERMS_REFUND: readonly string[] = [
  'If we cancel or cannot make the appointment, you get a full refund.',
  'Cancel 24 hours or more before your appointment and we refund you in full.',
  'Inside 24 hours, the assessment is not refunded — that includes a missed appointment.',
  `To cancel or reschedule, call or text ${SUPPORT_PHONE}.`,
];
```

Three things about that third line, none of them cosmetic:

- **"Inside 24 hours" is stated once and covers no-shows**, because the client's
  answer did not name no-shows separately and a missed appointment is by
  construction inside the window. Inventing a separate forfeiture clause would
  be inventing a policy.
- **The word "forfeit" does not appear.** It is a term of art that reads as
  punitive; "not refunded" says the same thing and is what the client said.
- **The company-cancel line comes first**, so the section opens on what we owe
  the customer rather than on what they lose. The old draft's failure mode was a
  block that ended on forfeiture; this one ends on the phone number, and the
  whole terms block still ends on `FEE_TERMS_CREDIT`.

A refund section that omitted customer cancellation would read as "fully
refundable" to a customer and "non-refundable" to us — the dispute this block
exists to prevent, and the reason the answer was worth waiting for.

**The review SLA is not published.** "Almost right away, max 1 hour" is
internal (client, 2026-08-16). `FEE_TERMS_INTRO` says "We review every request
before confirming it" and stops there.

**Tier 3 must state the lab-result turnaround** (client, 2026-08-18): *lab
results take 3-5 business days.* It goes in `FEE_TERMS_ITEMS[2]` itself, not in
a footnote — a customer choosing the $1,199 tier because they need documentation
is choosing on turnaround as much as on content, and a report they expected on
the day is a complaint.

**The weekend surcharge must be disclosed in the terms, not only in the live
price** (client, 2026-08-18: *"Yes weekend is extra"*). BK-31 shows the
multiplied number on the form; this ticket owns the sentence that makes it
expected rather than a surprise. One line in `FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT`, naming
Saturday and Sunday and the 1.5x, in plain words rather than as a formula.

**Mould pricing is deliberately NOT enumerated in the terms.** `FEE_TERMS_ITEMS`
carries the standard figures; mould customers see their own lower numbers in the
live price display. The terms therefore need one sentence saying the figures are
standard rates and the price shown for your job is the one that applies —
otherwise a mould customer reads $399 in the box and $385 beside the button and
has no way to know which is real. **This is the single most likely way this copy
ships subtly wrong**, because nothing about it looks like an error.

### Placement — the terms go BELOW the picker, not above it

**Added 2026-08-16.** On `/book/` the fee terms currently render in the page
header, *above* the booking island — so the first screen a visitor sees is the
friction, and the converting element (the calendar) is below it. The order is
backwards: the terms are the qualification on an offer that has not been made
yet.

Required order on `/book/`: **slot picker first, terms below or inside it.**

Two things make this a constraint rather than a preference:

- `FEE_TERMS_ACK_LABEL` says *"the assessment terms **above**"*. That is a claim
  about page order, and it is only true if the terms are above the checkbox —
  which they are inside step 3, and which is unaffected by moving the header
  copy below the picker. Keep both true. `verify-cutover.ts` already pins the
  terms-above-checkbox relationship; extend it rather than replace it.
- Step 3's box is the *acknowledged* copy and is not moving. The header block is
  the *informational* copy. After the move they must still be one set of
  constants — this ticket's whole premise — so the reorder is a template change,
  never a second copy of the strings.

### Where this departs from §5's draft, and why

Five departures. Each is drafting under the standing "implementer drafts,
client edits" rule; none invents policy.

1. **`FEE_TERMS_INTRO` drops §5's opening "Choose your assessment below."**
   §5 wrote it for one surface. It is false on the other three: the homepage
   card has no picker at all, `/book/`'s informational block is moving *below*
   the island (§3), and the email has no controls. Only the island's step-3 box
   has a chooser below the intro. A sentence that is true on one of four
   surfaces is the defect class this ticket exists to close.
   **This also lands BK-31's folded-in nit** — the price literal
   "starting at $399 + GST" is gone from INTRO, so `$399` now lives in
   `FEE_TERMS_ITEMS[0]` and in `TIER_DEFAULT_CENTS` and nowhere else.
2. **`FEE_TERMS_ITEMS[2]` carries the lab turnaround** (client, 2026-08-18),
   inside the bullet rather than as a footnote, in the same words
   `ASSESSMENT_TIER_DESCRIPTIONS.sketch` already ships — "Where lab samples are
   taken, results take 3–5 business days" — so the radio and the terms cannot
   drift into two different promises. En dash, matching the shipped constant.
3. **`FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT[0]` is the standard-rates sentence**, and it
   deliberately does **not** say "the price shown for your job is the one that
   applies". That phrasing, which the ticket suggests, is **not true of every
   job**: the 2026-08-18 amendments give the admin an adjustable amount at
   approval and a $1.15/km travel-fee field beyond 30 km, so a customer far out
   of town can be quoted more than the form showed. Writing the unqualified
   claim would manufacture exactly the written self-contradiction this ticket
   was opened to remove. The sentence does the job it was actually asked to do —
   a mould customer seeing `$385` beside the button and `$399` in the box now
   has the explanation — and `FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT[2]` names the approval email as
   where the amount is stated. **Flagged for the plan reviewer as the one place
   this plan declines the ticket's literal wording.**
4. **`FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT[1]` is the weekend sentence**, plain words, naming
   Saturday, Sunday and 1.5 (ticket requirement). It is a *second sentence*
   about the same fact as `AFTER_HOURS_NOTE`, not a second copy of that string:
   `AFTER_HOURS_NOTE` is a just-in-time explanation gated on
   `tierQuotes[…].afterHours` and rendered beside the inflated figure, and the
   terms need the fact unconditionally, on surfaces where no price renders at
   all. A new verify assertion (§4) pins that both agree with
   `AFTER_HOURS_NUMERATOR / AFTER_HOURS_DENOMINATOR`, so the two sentences
   cannot drift to two different multipliers.
5. **`FEE_TERMS_REFUND[3]` is `CANCEL_LINE`, the existing constant**, whose

   keeps the phone number in one place. Consequence, accepted: in the two

   section, once as the message's own closing instruction at
   `booking-email.ts:390`. Left as-is rather than suppressed, because dropping
   the message-level line would delete it from the confirmation entirely and
   because the terms block has to be self-contained on the homepage card, where
   the message-level line does not exist.

## The four blockers, and what each would have shipped

### B1 — the payment sentences are false on the pay-now branch

`FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT[2]` said the link email states *"the date it has to be paid
by"* and `[3]` said *"If it is not paid by then we release the time."* Both are
false whenever approval takes the pay-now branch, and that branch is routine,
not exotic:

- `booking-review.ts:47-48` — `slot − now < PAY_NOW_THRESHOLD_HOURS` returns
  `{ dueAt: null, payNow: true }`.
- `booking-email.ts:516, 549-553` — `dueAt === null` renders
  `APPROVED_PAY_NOW_NOTE` instead of *"Please pay by …"*. **No date is stated.**
- `booking-copy.ts:368-369` — that note says the opposite of `[3]`: *"we hold
  the time until you do."*
- `booking-review.ts:66-68` — `isPaymentOverdue(null) === false`, so the cron
  never releases the slot. `booking-payment.ts:186-193` says so outright.

`PAY_NOW_THRESHOLD_HOURS = 8`, every admin/phone/emergency row bypasses the
notice rule, and P9's own worked example puts an ordinary web booking on this

are actually true — in the approval email, per branch.
