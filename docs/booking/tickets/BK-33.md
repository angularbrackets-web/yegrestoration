## Scope

- `stripe.refunds.create({ payment_intent, amount?, reason })`, idempotency key
  `refund-<appointment_id>-<attempt>`.
- **Company-cancel refund is one action.** When the office moves a paid row to
  `cancelled`, a *Refund in full* checkbox appears, defaulted **ON** when a
  company-side reason is selected. This is the path that must be impossible to
  forget: a customer who paid for a visit we cancelled and then had to phone to
  chase their money is the worst outcome this feature has.
- The `amount` parameter is supported even if the policy turns out to be
  all-or-nothing. Partial refunds cost nothing to support now and a migration
  later.
- Columns: `stripe_refund_id TEXT`, `refunded_amount_cents INTEGER`,
  `refunded_at TIMESTAMPTZ`; `payment_status` gains `refunded` /
  `partially_refunded` (already in BK-32's CHECK).
- Webhook handles `charge.refunded` and `refund.updated` — **a refund issued
  from the Stripe dashboard by the office must reach our row too.** The office
  will use the dashboard; assuming otherwise is how the two records diverge.

## Policy constants — answered 2026-08-18

**Client answer, verbatim in effect:** *full refund if cancelled 24+ hours
before the appointment; no refund within 24 hours.* Exceptions are case-by-case
and handled **manually in the Stripe dashboard** — no code.

In `booking-config.ts`:

```ts
REFUND_COMPANY_CANCEL        = 'full'   // certain, and always was
REFUND_CUSTOMER_CANCEL_HOURS = 24       // client, 2026-08-18
REFUND_CUSTOMER_CANCEL_AFTER = 'none'   // client, 2026-08-18
```

`REFUND_NO_SHOW` **is deliberately not a constant.** The client's answer did not
name no-shows separately, and a no-show is by construction inside the 24-hour
window — so it is already covered by `REFUND_CUSTOMER_CANCEL_AFTER`. Adding a
third constant would invent a policy nobody stated. BK-36 words it as the 24-hour
rule rather than as forfeiture.

The `null`-renders-as-"call us" machinery the draft specified is **no longer
needed for these values** and should not be built for them. Keep the shape in
mind only if a future value is genuinely unknown.

## Out of scope

- Enforcing the policy automatically. The constants describe what the office
  owes; **every refund still starts with a human** and the 24-hour rule is not
  wired to a timer. There is no self-service cancel to hang one off.
- Automatic refunds on customer cancellation — there is no self-service cancel
  (locked, phone-in), so every refund starts with a human.
- The cron/webhook race refund case, which BK-32 deliberately resolves as
  "flag, do not auto-refund".

## Risk areas for the reviewer

Money movement, double refunds under webhook replay, dashboard-issued refunds
reconciling correctly, and the company-cancel default not being silently
flipped off.

## Decisions — user, 2026-08-20, and one of them overturns the Scope above

Four questions were asked before planning, because the draft's UI was written on
2026-08-16 against a screen BK-46 has since rebuilt.

1. **ONE action.** A dedicated **"Cancel and refund"** panel on a paid row:
   it refunds in Stripe and cancels the booking in one operation. The status
   dropdown keeps plain cancel for unpaid rows and for the rare
   cancel-without-refund.

   **This supersedes the Scope bullet "a *Refund in full* checkbox appears,
   defaulted ON when a company-side reason is selected."** That bullet is dead
   and the reasons are recorded rather than left implicit: there is no
   company-side *reason* field anywhere in this system to default off, and the
   only cancel control today is the status dropdown inside the **"Update the
   record"** form — the form whose own on-screen line reads *"Record-keeping,
   not a decision."* Putting a money-moving control in it would re-create
   exactly the panel ambiguity BK-44 was filed to remove. The *intent* of the
   bullet survives intact and is the design driver: the refund must be
   impossible to forget.

2. **The cancellation email names the refund — only when the customer paid and
   only when we are refunding.** A refund paragraph on that arm alone; a
   cancellation with no refund keeps today's copy byte-for-byte.

3. **The office sees the REAL processing fee from Stripe**, not an estimate and
   not a silent omission.

4. **The refund amount is an editable field.** Partial refunds are issued from
   this screen, not only from the dashboard.

**Decision 4 forces decision 2's content.** With partial refunds reachable from
the screen, an email saying *"a refund has been issued"* with no figure is
actively misleading to a customer refunded $300 of $628.43. **The email names
the amount.** This is a consequence, not a second product choice.

### Task 1 — The gateway grows two calls

`StripeGateway` gains, alongside `createSession` / `expireSession` /
`sessionStatus`:

```ts
createRefund: (params, idempotencyKey) => Promise<{ id: string; amount: number; status: string | null }>;
chargeFee:    (paymentIntentId: string) => Promise<number | null>;
```

Through the same injectable seam as every other external call in the module.
**That seam is the only reason a refund can be red-first at all**, and a refund
is the worst possible place to relearn the module header's lesson.

`chargeFee` is `paymentIntents.retrieve(id, { expand:
['latest_charge.balance_transaction'] })` reading `.fee`. Verified against
`stripe@22.5.0`'s own `.d.ts`: `PaymentIntent.latest_charge` is
`string | Charge | null`, `Charge.balance_transaction` is
`string | BalanceTransaction | null`, `BalanceTransaction.fee` is `number`,
`PaymentIntentRetrieveParams.expand` exists.

**No `reason` is sent** *(S7 — the Scope's `refunds.create({ ..., reason })` is
dropped, deliberately and on the record)*. `RefundCreateParams.Reason` is only
`'duplicate' | 'fraudulent' | 'requested_by_customer'`. None describes a company
cancellation; `'requested_by_customer'` is false on the path this ticket exists
for; and `'fraudulent'` **adds the card and the email to Stripe Radar's block
lists**, which would quietly ban a customer we cancelled on.

**`metadata: { appointment_id, source: 'admin-refund' }` IS sent** — not as
control flow (see Task 5's flag rule) but so the refund is identifiable in the
dashboard by the person reading it there.

**The fee does not scale with a partial refund.** Stripe returns no part of the
processing fee on any refund, so the cost is the same $18.52 whether $628.43 or
$50 goes back. Copy says *the fee on the original charge is not returned*, never
"the fee on this refund".

### Task 2 — `refundPayment()`, and the dedupe lives in the DATABASE *(B2)*

`markPaid`'s sibling in `booking-payment.ts`: **never throws**, outcome union,
guarded UPDATEs, `StripeDeps & NotifyDeps`.

**Three phases, and the middle one is the only one that moves money.**

**Phase 1 — CLAIM THE ATTEMPT. Writes no money state.** This is not the
claim-then-refund the first draft correctly rejected: nothing written says money
went back, so a Stripe failure leaves no lie on the row.

```sql
UPDATE appointments
   SET refund_claim_key = $key, refund_started_at = $now, updated_at = $now
 WHERE id = $id
   AND payment_method = 'stripe'
   AND stripe_payment_intent_id IS NOT NULL
   AND payment_status IN ('paid', 'partially_refunded')
   AND COALESCE(refunded_amount_cents, 0) = $alreadyRefundedAtRequestTime
   AND refund_claim_key IS NULL
RETURNING *
```

`refund_claim_key IS NULL` makes the claim **exclusive**, which is what closes
the hole the first draft admitted and under-rated: two office members refunding
**different** amounts that sum to less than the charge produced two keys and two
real refunds, with Stripe's over-refund refusal — the draft's stated backstop —
never firing at all.

Zero rows is read back and classified, never collapsed: `already-refunding`
(a claim is open — a double-click, a second tab, or a genuine race) ·
`not-card` · `no-charge` · `not-paid` · `changed-underneath` (the refundable
balance moved between the confirm screen and the click) · `missing`.

**Phase 2 — Stripe.** `refunds.create` with idempotency key
`refund-<id>-<alreadyRefunded>-<amount>`. Kept as **defence in depth only**;
per the documentation above it is not a dedupe and the plan no longer claims it
is.

**Phase 3 — SETTLE.** One guarded UPDATE writing `stripe_refund_id`,
`refunded_amount_cents`, `refunded_at`, `payment_status`, `status = 'cancelled'`
and `cancelled_at`, **and clearing `refund_claim_key` back to NULL** so a later
deliberate partial refund can claim. Guarded on the claim still being ours, so a
replay settles once.

**Claim release, by failure mode, and the two directions are not the same:**

- Stripe **rejected** the call (a definite answer — over-refund, wrong state,
  invalid amount): clear the claim. Nothing moved and the office may retry.
- Stripe **timed out or the answer is unknown**: **leave the claim standing**
  and flag. Refusing further refunds until a human looks is the safe direction,
  and `charge.refunded` reconciles the row if the money did in fact move.
- Phase 3 fails after Stripe succeeded: outcome `refunded-not-cancelled`,
  flagged loudly *(S9 — and the flash names the recovery: the status dropdown
  still permits `confirmed → cancelled` on a refunded row, because `cancelled`
  is not invite-holding and no crossing-in guard applies)*.

Outcomes: `refunded` · `partially-refunded` · `already-refunding` ·
`refunded-not-cancelled` · `not-card` · `no-charge` · `not-paid` ·
`changed-underneath` · `too-much` · `invalid-amount` · `stripe-error` ·
`stripe-unknown` · `missing` · `error`.

***(S3)*** **What decides `refunded` from `partially_refunded`:** on this path,
the new total refunded against **`paid_amount_cents`** — what actually
arrived — never `total_amount_cents`, which `db.ts:170-175` is explicit is the
quote. On the webhook path, `charge.amount_refunded` against `charge.amount`.

### Task 3 — The admin route and the two-step confirm

New sibling endpoint `api/admin/appointments/refund.ts`. Verified: that
directory holds only `create` / `file-delete` / `mark-paid` / `resend` /
`review` / `update`, none dynamic, and the segment carries no dot, so neither
the dynamic-route collision nor the trailing-slash 308 applies. Pinned against
the **generated** route table, not argued for in a comment.

Two steps, mirroring `review.ts`'s `preview` → `approve`, because this is
irreversible and because the real fee must be on screen before the click that
moves money:

- `action=preview` — parse the typed amount with the same parser the refund
  re-parses, call `chargeFee`, bounce back.
- The confirm panel renders the amount, what is left refundable, the real fee
  Stripe keeps, and that the booking will be cancelled and the customer emailed.
  ***(N5)*** the query parameter is `refundconfirm`, **not** `confirm`, which is
  `review.ts`'s — two amber panels about the same money is already on the
  ROADMAP as BK-47's.
- `action=refund` — `refundPayment()`. ***(S10)*** the refundable balance is
  **re-derived server-side** at this step and re-checked inside Phase 1's WHERE;
  the query string is display only and is trusted for nothing.

If `chargeFee` fails or returns null the confirm step **still renders**, with
the fee line degraded to the no-number sentence. A failed Stripe *read* must not
block a refund the office needs to issue.

**The panel renders on** a row whose `payment_method = 'stripe'`, whose
`stripe_payment_intent_id` is non-null, and whose `payment_status` is `paid` or
`partially_refunded` — **which includes rows already `cancelled`, `declined` or
`payment_expired`** carrying money from `markPaid`'s paid-after-release branch.
Stating that is not housekeeping; it is what makes B7 below a decision rather
than an accident.

### Task 4 — One cancellation path, and the refund notice is NOT gated on the calendar *(B7, S1, S2)*

`sendBoundaryMail` is extracted from `update.ts` into `booking-admin-notify.ts`
and both routes call it. ***(S1)*** it gains a **`NotifyDeps` parameter** in the

***(B7)*** **The refund notice is a money message and is not hung off the
invite boundary.** `sendBoundaryMail` returns early when `hadInvite ===
hasInvite`, so a cancel-and-refund on a row `markPaid` recorded as
`paid-after-release` — already `cancelled`, carrying real money — crosses no

Decision 2 exists for. So:

- Boundary crossed → **one** message: the cancellation, carrying the refund

- No boundary crossed → the refund notice alone, no ics.
- **One builder, two arms**, parameterised on whether the cancellation clause

***(S2)*** **`CANCELLED_LEAD`'s *"as requested"* is not carried onto the refund

### Task 5 — Reconciliation, and the flag asks the ROW, not the event *(B1, B6, S8, N2, N3, N4)*

***(S8)*** **`plannedAction` is not the reconciliation.** `handle()` returns
early for any event outside the three checkout types, so new `plannedAction`
arms would be **inert while `verify-stripe-webhook.ts` stayed green** — the
"assertion that cannot fail" family, which this repo has now caught eight times.
So the work lands in an exported `reconcileRefund()`, a `markPaid` sibling
(never throws, guarded UPDATE), `handle()`'s narrowing is rewritten to reach it,
and it is asserted **against a real row** in `verify-booking-admin-db.ts`, not
only as a routing decision. *(This also settles the first draft's own confusion:
the webhook is not a caller of `refundPayment()` — reconciliation creates no
refund.)*

| Event | Object | Handled how |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `charge.refunded` | `Charge` | **reconcile** — `amount_refunded` is Stripe's running total and is the figure written |
| `refund.updated` | `Refund` | reconcile on `succeeded`; **flag only** on `failed` / `canceled` |
| `refund.failed` | `Refund` | **flag only** |
| `charge.refund.updated` | `Refund` | same handling ***(N4 — the SDK documents this as "a refund updated on selected payment methods", not as a legacy twin; handling both is idempotent, but the first draft's justification was wrong)*** |
| `refund.created` | `Refund` | ignored — `charge.refunded` carries the running total, and acting on both races two events for one row |

Row found by `stripe_payment_intent_id`. ***(N3)*** a `payment_intent` no row
carries is a logged no-op, in the shape `appointmentIdOf` already uses.

***(B1)*** **THE FLAG IS CONDITIONAL, AND THIS IS THE FIX FOR THE HAPPY PATH.**
The first draft flagged every reconciled refund with *"still CONFIRMED and still
holding its slot"* — so **every successful screen-issued refund would have ended
in a permanent red banner saying the opposite of what happened**, because
`charge.refunded` can arrive before Phase 3 lands, `needs_attention` is
append-only and nothing clears it, and `[id].astro` renders it in red above
everything with a badge on the list row. The flag now asks two questions of the
row: is `refund_claim_key` non-null (our refund is in flight — say nothing, our
own path is about to cancel), and is the row still in a slot-holding status? It
flags only a refund that arrived from outside and left a booking standing.
***(N2)*** and the text says what the office now owes, **including that the
customer has not been told about the money** — which they cannot infer from
"still confirmed".

**Never cancels, never emails.** A dashboard refund says something about money
and nothing about whether the visit is happening.

***(B6)*** **`refund.failed` writes NO money state — flag only.** The first
draft restored `payment_status = 'paid'` and NULLed the refund columns when the
failed refund's id matched `stripe_refund_id`. `stripe_refund_id` holds **one**
id and a charge can carry several refunds: refund A of $300 succeeds, refund B
is issued and its id replaces A's, B fails, the guard matches — and the arm
erases a $300 refund that genuinely went back, restoring *"Payment received —
Paid by card"*. **That is this ticket's headline defect, recreated by this
ticket's own code.** Stripe lowers `charge.amount_refunded` when a refund fails
and the corrected total arrives on the charge; any correction comes from that
total or from a human, never from clearing.

***(B3)*** **The obvious fix to the late-payment branch converts an arrival of
real money into silence.** `NOT IN ('paid','refunded','partially_refunded')`
makes the UPDATE match zero rows on a refunded row and the function returns
`'not-applicable'` **above** the `alertOffice(...)` call — money lands on a
refunded, released booking and nothing is written and nobody is told, which is
the "money taken, nothing flagged" outcome the webhook's own three-layer design
exists to prevent. So the refunded case gets its **own arm**: money columns
untouched, `needs_attention` appended, `alertOffice` called. The append carries
the payment's own reference as a marker and the UPDATE guards on
`needs_attention` not already containing it, so a replayed event flags once.

### Task 6b — The rule rewrite, re-derived *(B4, B5)*

***(B4)*** **The predicate is NOT `refunded_at IS NULL`.** Nothing clears
`refunded_at` — not `approve`, not `rollBack`, not `markPaid` — so a booking
refunded, walked back to `pending_review`, re-approved and **genuinely re-paid**
would be permanently barred from the invite-holding set while every screen
showed it as paid: two records diverged forever with no route back. BK-46
survived this exact trap by keying on `payment_status`, the column the payment
path actually maintains, and its reasoning is written out in `booking-admin.ts`.
This follows it.

- **Old:** may cross into the invite-holding set only if `paid_at IS NOT NULL`.
- **New:** may cross in only if `paid_at IS NOT NULL` **AND `payment_status NOT
  IN ('refunded','partially_refunded')`.**

And **belt and braces**: `markPaid`'s confirm UPDATE clears
`stripe_refund_id` / `refunded_amount_cents` / `refunded_at` /
`refund_claim_key`, so a re-paid row carries no previous cycle's refund in any
column a query can reach. `approve` and `rollBack` are left alone — that is the
never-cleared-columns ticket's job, recorded below.

| Case | Old | New | Changed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cancelled` → `confirmed`, paid, never refunded | allow | allow | no |
| `cancelled` → `confirmed`, paid then **refunded in full** | allow | **refuse** | **YES — the hole** |
| `cancelled` → `confirmed`, paid then **partially refunded** | allow | **refuse** | **YES** — the row no longer holds the money it was confirmed against, and re-confirming it is a decision with a number attached, which a dropdown cannot carry |
| `declined`/`payment_expired` → `confirmed`, late payment stamped, refunded since | allow | refuse | YES, same reason |
| pre-P9 legacy `cancelled` → `confirmed` (`paid_at` NULL) | refuse | refuse | no — `payment_status` on legacy rows is `not_required`, so the new clause is inert |
| `completed` ⇄ `confirmed` ⇄ `no_show` on a refunded row | allow | allow | no — no crossing, no mail. **Deliberate**: a refunded visit that went ahead anyway must still be recordable |
| `pending_review` → `completed` on a paid-then-refunded row | refuse | refuse | no, but **the REASON changes** from "unpaid" to "refunded", which is what S5 makes the screen say |
| ***(B5)*** refunded → `pending_review` → re-approved → **re-paid** → later crossing back in | allow | **allow** | **no — and only because of the `payment_status` keying and the `markPaid` clear above.** Under `refunded_at IS NULL` this became *refuse, forever*, and nobody had decided that |
| ***(B5)*** **`confirmed` row refunded from the DASHBOARD** (Task 5 refuses to cancel) | — | movement within the set stays allowed | **the first draft never enumerated a refunded row still `confirmed`, yet Task 5 guarantees they exist** |
| ***(B5)*** `confirmed`(refunded) → `cancelled` → `confirmed` | allow | **refuse** | **YES** — follows from the row above, and was unstated |
| anything → `approved_awaiting_payment` | refuse | refuse | no |
| `approved_awaiting_payment` → `pending_review`, live session | refuse | refuse | no |
| `approved_awaiting_payment` → invite-holding | refuse | refuse | no |
| anything → `cancelled` / `declined` / `payment_expired` | allow | allow | no — **and S9 depends on this staying true** |

`EditorGuardRow` gains `payment_status`; `update.ts`'s WHERE gains the clause.
***(S5)*** the dropdown's withheld sentences are **partitioned, not appended
to**: `untilPaidWithheld` currently renders *"Not listed until this booking is
paid"* over every withheld invite-holding status, which is false of a row that
**was** paid and refunded. A fourth line is not enough — the two must not fire

### Task 7 — Negative pins, and what they actually match *(B9)*

***(B9)*** **The existing pins cover only the arm this ticket is not changing.**
`verify-booking-ics.ts:749-751` runs its `BANNED` loop — policy number, claim
number, `GST_REGISTRATION_NUMBER`, a have-ready item — plus `!/\$\d/` and
`!/https?:\/\//` over `planCancellationEmail(EVENT, CUSTOMER, NOW)`, the
no-refund call. With the refund argument optional **every one of those stays
green over the new arm and covers nothing**: no PII pin, no URL pin, no amount
pin. `verify-booking-ics.ts` was not in the first draft's list of scripts to

## Acceptance criteria

Written at plan review, 2026-08-20, as observables rather than implementation.

**Money movement**

1. A refund issued from the screen moves exactly the typed amount, once, and the
   row afterwards reports a total-refunded figure equal to Stripe's own
   `amount_refunded` for that charge.
2. Two submissions of the same refund — double-click, second tab, browser
   retry — result in **one** refund at Stripe and one recorded total. Driven
   through the injected gateway with a fake that counts calls.
3. Two submissions of **different** amounts, concurrently, result in at most one
   refund; the loser reports that a refund is already in flight and moves no
   money.
4. A refund exceeding what is left refundable moves nothing and says so; the row
   is unchanged.
5. A Stripe error moves nothing, writes no refund column, does not cancel the
   booking, and does not email the customer.
6. A refund on a non-card row (Interac / onsite / none / no payment intent)
   refuses, names the reason, and directs the office to the bank.

**The two records**

7. When the Stripe call succeeds and the database write fails, the row is
   flagged loudly, the outcome names *money went back, booking not cancelled*,
   and the office is given a control that cancels without attempting a second
   refund.
8. After **any** successful refund — screen or dashboard, full or partial, first
   or repeated — the row's refunded total equals Stripe's, and the flag on the
   row does **not** assert a state contradicted by the row itself. In particular
   a screen refund that succeeded and cancelled leaves **no** standing claim that
   the booking is still confirmed and holding its slot.

   **RESTATED AT IMPLEMENTATION REVIEW, because the first half is not true
   unconditionally and stating it flatly hid a real residual.** `reconcileRefund`
   writes the total carried by the event it is handed, and Stripe does not
   guarantee event order. Two `charge.refunded` events delivered out of sequence
   leave the row on whichever was processed last, and in the terminal case — a
   delayed event for an earlier, smaller refund arriving after a later one — the
   row reports the smaller figure permanently, with `oursInFlight` false and the
   booking already released, so **no flag either**. Stripe protects the money;
   the record diverges quietly, which is the class of defect this ticket exists
   to remove.

   The fix in the same spirit as "Stripe's latest word wins" is to retrieve the
   charge in the webhook arm and write its CURRENT `amount_refunded` rather than
   the payload's snapshot — same authority, no ordering assumption. **Not done
   here**: it adds a Stripe call and a failure mode to the webhook, which is a
   change worth its own consideration rather than a late edit to a money path.
   Recorded in ROADMAP Known traps with an owner. The second half of this
   criterion — no standing "still confirmed" claim on the happy path — is met and
   pinned (red row R11).
9. A redelivered `charge.refunded` changes no figure and adds no second flag.
10. A refund that **fails** at Stripe leaves the row's recorded refund totals as
    Stripe reports them, and never restores "Payment received" over money that
    did go back on some earlier refund.
11. Nothing in this ticket writes `payment_status = 'paid'`, `paid_at` or
    `payment_method` on a row whose money has been refunded — including when a
    late payment arrives on such a row, which must still flag and still alert the
    office.

**What the screens say**

12. On a fully refunded row, no admin surface states "Payment received", "Paid
    by card", or any present-tense claim that we hold the money. Asserted on the
    **rendered** screen, over a claim shape, not over a constant name.
13. On a partially refunded row, the screen names both figures — what was
    refunded and what was originally paid — and never presents the refunded
    amount as the amount held.
14. On a **paid, non-refunded** row, no surface claims a refund. The mirror pin
    fires.
15. The status dropdown offers no transition that would put a refunded booking
    back into the invite-holding set, and the sentence explaining the omission is
    true of *that* row — specifically it does not say "until this booking is
    paid" about a booking that was paid and refunded.
16. A row that was refunded on a previous cycle, re-approved and **re-paid** is a
    fully normal confirmed booking: the office can still move it to `completed` /
    `no_show` / `cancelled` and back, and the screen states the current payment,
    not the previous cycle's refund.

**What the customer receives**

17. The refund arm of the cancellation email names the refunded **amount** and
    the 5–10 business-day timing, does not claim the money is back today, and
    does not claim we still hold it.
18. The refund arm carries no URL, no policy number, no claim number, no GST
    registration number, no have-ready list — the same guards the no-refund arm
    already carries, asserted on the refund arm itself.
19. When only part of the payment was refunded, the booking's full total appears
    nowhere in that email.
20. A cancellation with **no** refund is byte-identical to today's message.
21. A refund on a booking that is already cancelled still tells the customer
    their money is coming back; the notice is not skipped because no calendar
    boundary was crossed.

**Reconciliation from the dashboard**

22. A refund taken in the Stripe dashboard reaches the row without any admin
    action: the recorded total matches Stripe's, and the row is flagged with what
    the office must now do — including that the customer has not been told.
23. That path never cancels the booking, never sends the customer anything, and
    never releases the slot.
24. **Proved by artifact, not by configuration:** a real refund taken in the live
    Stripe dashboard on a real row, observed reconciling. The ticket is not
    closed on the event types having been added to the endpoint.

**Method gates**

25. Every one of the above that can fail has been **seen red** through the
    injected `StripeGateway` / `NotifyDeps` — success, over-refund refusal, API
    error, hang, and a failed database write — with one gate-table entry per
    failure mode. No guard in this ticket is evidenced by reading source.
26. `npm run typecheck` and `npm run build` green, and **all** verify scripts
    green — including `verify-booking-ics.ts`, whose cancellation-email pins this
    ticket changes.
27. The new endpoint is proved reachable against the **generated**
    `.vercel/output/config.json`, not against `astro dev`.

## The 2026-08-22 copy revision — user decision on the refund email

The refund arm's opening line was the one item BK-33 shipped to commit without
the user having read it (recorded as open question 1 in the session handoff).
Asked on 2026-08-22, the user **kept the dropped "as requested"** — the reason
holds — and **required one change**: the leads must not close with *"Nothing
further is needed from you."*

**The reason is that it argues with the two sentences under it.** The refund
arms carry `refundedAmountLine` and `REFUND_TIMING_LINE`, and the second asks the
customer to watch their statement for five to ten business days. A lead telling
them there is nothing to do, two lines above an instruction to watch for
something, is a message contradicting itself — and it does so on the one
message in this system that is about a customer's returned money.

**What was already there, and what was actually missing.** Half of the change
the user asked for was in the message before this edit: `REFUND_TIMING_LINE`
already names the statement and the ten-day window, added off the 2026-08-20
live cycle for exactly that reason. What was missing was the other half —
**what to do when the window passes.** The only phone line in the message is
`CANCELLED_REBOOK_LINE`, offered under *"if this cancellation is a surprise"*,
which the customer whose refund has not landed on day twelve will not read as
addressed to them.

So the edit is three things, and deliberately not a rewrite of the timing line:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `CANCELLED_REFUNDED_LEAD` | loses the sentence — *"We've cancelled the assessment below and sent your payment back."* |
| `REFUNDED_LEAD` | loses it too — *"We've sent back your payment for the assessment below."* |
| `REFUND_CHASE_LINE` **(new)** | *"If it hasn't reached you by then, call or text (780) 479-3285 and we'll look it up."* — appended as the third refund line |

**"By then" is load-bearing and is pinned as such.** It binds the new sentence
to `REFUND_TIMING_LINE` above it. An unanchored *"call us if it doesn't
arrive"* placed in the lead — the alternative wording considered — invites the
call on the same evening, which is the exact call `REFUND_TIMING_LINE` exists to
prevent. A pin asserts the chase line appears AFTER the timing line, so a later
reordering breaks the anaphora loudly rather than silently.

`SUPPORT_PHONE` now appears twice in the message, once per reason. Deliberate:
the reasons are different, and a reader scanning for their money should not have
to map it onto a sentence about rebooking.

**`CANCELLED_LEAD` is unchanged** — the no-refund cancellation is still
byte-identical to what shipped before BK-33, and the existing pin holds it there.

## What the 2026-08-20 live cycle proved about this ticket

Booking #36 was charged $628.43 on a real card, refunded in the Stripe
dashboard, then cancelled through the admin panel. Everything below is observed,
not predicted, and it sharpens the scope above rather than changing it.

### The reconciliation gap is real and now VISIBLE

The draft already said *"a refund issued from the Stripe dashboard by the office
must reach our row too."* Confirmed: nothing in `src/` calls `refunds.create`,
the webhook handles no refund event, and after a full dashboard refund the row
still reads `payment_status = 'paid'` with `paid_at` stamped.

**What changed on 2026-08-20 is the severity, not the fact.** BK-46 gave
`paid_at` and `payment_method` their first reader — a **"Payment received —
Paid by card"** panel. Before that the stale state was silent; now the screen
states it confidently on a booking that was refunded in full. Giving those
columns a voice also gave the wrong answer one.

### The operational procedure this replaces, recorded so the ticket knows what "better" means

Today the office must do **two things in two systems, in this order**:

1. **Refund in Stripe first.** Refund-first fails safe: interrupted, the
   customer has their money and a live booking, which a phone call fixes.
   Cancel-first leaves them told the booking is off while we still hold $628 —
   and `CANCELLED_LEAD` says *"nothing further is needed from you"* and mentions
   money not at all, so they are given no reason to expect a refund.
2. **Then cancel through the admin panel** — never by deleting the row, which
   bypasses the CANCEL invite and strands the appointment on the customer's
   calendar.

**Both steps are unenforced and unrecorded.** Miss step 2 and the slot stays
blocked; miss step 1 and nothing anywhere says so.

### Three facts for the mechanism

- **Stripe does not return the processing fee on a refund.** #36: $628.43
  refunded, `-$18.52` fees kept, net `-$18.52`. So a company-side cancellation
  costs the business the fee, every time. Whether that is worth surfacing to the
  office at the moment they click is a product question this ticket should ask.
- **A refund takes 5–10 business days to reach the customer's statement**, while
  Stripe shows it immediately. Any copy this ticket writes must not imply the
  money is back today.
- **The customer gets no notice from us.** Stripe may email its own refund
  notification; our cancellation email says nothing about money. A customer who
  paid $628 and was cancelled on hears about their refund only from Stripe, in a
  message we do not control and cannot see.

### Scope this suggests adding

- **Say what happened, in our own words.** If the office refunds through this
  ticket's action, the cancellation email should name the refund and its
  timing — the one message we control that a cancelled customer definitely
  reads.
- **The dashboard path must still reconcile.** The office will sometimes refund
  in Stripe directly, and the webhook is the only thing that can catch it. This
  was already in scope; today it is evidenced.
- **The 24-hour policy is a decision, not an automation.** The client's answer
  (full refund 24+ hours before, none inside) is a rule the office applies. This
  ticket should make the correct action obvious at the moment of clicking, not
  refuse the other one — a company-side cancellation is a full refund whatever
  the clock says.
