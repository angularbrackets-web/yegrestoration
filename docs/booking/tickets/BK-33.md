at the end.** Mechanism unchanged. **Next ticket to build.**

**Deploy:** 3. **Migration:** folds into 008 if BK-32 has not shipped, else its

**Mechanism only, and it stays policy-free by design** — the office can always
refund by hand, so nothing here ever waited on the policy. As of 2026-08-18 the
policy is answered anyway, which changes the constants below and nothing else.

## Scope

- `stripe.refunds.create({ payment_intent, amount?, reason })`, idempotency key
  `refund-<appointment_id>-<attempt>`.
- **Company-cancel refund is one action.** When the office moves a paid row to
  `cancelled`, a *Refund in full* checkbox appears, defaulted **ON** when a
  company-side reason is selected. This is the path that must be impossible to
  forget: a customer who paid for a visit we cancelled and then had to phone to
  chase their money is the worst outcome this feature has.
- The `amount` parameter is supported even if the policy turns out to be
  all-or-nothing. Partial refunds cost nothing to support now and a migration
  later.
- Columns: `stripe_refund_id TEXT`, `refunded_amount_cents INTEGER`,
  `refunded_at TIMESTAMPTZ`; `payment_status` gains `refunded` /
  `partially_refunded` (already in BK-32's CHECK).
- Webhook handles `charge.refunded` and `refund.updated` — **a refund issued
  from the Stripe dashboard by the office must reach our row too.** The office
  will use the dashboard; assuming otherwise is how the two records diverge.

## Policy constants — answered 2026-08-18

**Client answer, verbatim in effect:** *full refund if cancelled 24+ hours
before the appointment; no refund within 24 hours.* Exceptions are case-by-case
and handled **manually in the Stripe dashboard** — no code.

In `booking-config.ts`:

```ts
REFUND_COMPANY_CANCEL        = 'full'   // certain, and always was
REFUND_CUSTOMER_CANCEL_HOURS = 24       // client, 2026-08-18
REFUND_CUSTOMER_CANCEL_AFTER = 'none'   // client, 2026-08-18
```

`REFUND_NO_SHOW` **is deliberately not a constant.** The client's answer did not
name no-shows separately, and a no-show is by construction inside the 24-hour
window — so it is already covered by `REFUND_CUSTOMER_CANCEL_AFTER`. Adding a
third constant would invent a policy nobody stated. BK-36 words it as the 24-hour
rule rather than as forfeiture.

The `null`-renders-as-"call us" machinery the draft specified is **no longer
needed for these values** and should not be built for them. Keep the shape in
mind only if a future value is genuinely unknown.

## Out of scope

- Enforcing the policy automatically. The constants describe what the office
  owes; **every refund still starts with a human** and the 24-hour rule is not
  wired to a timer. There is no self-service cancel to hang one off.
- Automatic refunds on customer cancellation — there is no self-service cancel
  (locked, phone-in), so every refund starts with a human.
- The cron/webhook race refund case, which BK-32 deliberately resolves as
  "flag, do not auto-refund".

## Risk areas for the reviewer

Money movement, double refunds under webhook replay, dashboard-issued refunds
reconciling correctly, and the company-cancel default not being silently
flipped off.

## What the 2026-08-20 live cycle proved about this ticket

Booking #36 was charged $628.43 on a real card, refunded in the Stripe
dashboard, then cancelled through the admin panel. Everything below is observed,
not predicted, and it sharpens the scope above rather than changing it.

### The reconciliation gap is real and now VISIBLE

The draft already said *"a refund issued from the Stripe dashboard by the office
must reach our row too."* Confirmed: nothing in `src/` calls `refunds.create`,
the webhook handles no refund event, and after a full dashboard refund the row
still reads `payment_status = 'paid'` with `paid_at` stamped.

**What changed on 2026-08-20 is the severity, not the fact.** BK-46 gave
`paid_at` and `payment_method` their first reader — a **"Payment received —
Paid by card"** panel. Before that the stale state was silent; now the screen
states it confidently on a booking that was refunded in full. Giving those
columns a voice also gave the wrong answer one.

### The operational procedure this replaces, recorded so the ticket knows what "better" means

Today the office must do **two things in two systems, in this order**:

1. **Refund in Stripe first.** Refund-first fails safe: interrupted, the
   customer has their money and a live booking, which a phone call fixes.
   Cancel-first leaves them told the booking is off while we still hold $628 —
   and `CANCELLED_LEAD` says *"nothing further is needed from you"* and mentions
   money not at all, so they are given no reason to expect a refund.
2. **Then cancel through the admin panel** — never by deleting the row, which
   bypasses the CANCEL invite and strands the appointment on the customer's
   calendar.

**Both steps are unenforced and unrecorded.** Miss step 2 and the slot stays
blocked; miss step 1 and nothing anywhere says so.

### Three facts for the mechanism

- **Stripe does not return the processing fee on a refund.** #36: $628.43
  refunded, `-$18.52` fees kept, net `-$18.52`. So a company-side cancellation
  costs the business the fee, every time. Whether that is worth surfacing to the
  office at the moment they click is a product question this ticket should ask.
- **A refund takes 5–10 business days to reach the customer's statement**, while
  Stripe shows it immediately. Any copy this ticket writes must not imply the
  money is back today.
- **The customer gets no notice from us.** Stripe may email its own refund
  notification; our cancellation email says nothing about money. A customer who
  paid $628 and was cancelled on hears about their refund only from Stripe, in a
  message we do not control and cannot see.

### Scope this suggests adding

- **Say what happened, in our own words.** If the office refunds through this
  ticket's action, the cancellation email should name the refund and its
  timing — the one message we control that a cancelled customer definitely
  reads.
- **The dashboard path must still reconcile.** The office will sometimes refund
  in Stripe directly, and the webhook is the only thing that can catch it. This
  was already in scope; today it is evidenced.
- **The 24-hour policy is a decision, not an automation.** The client's answer
  (full refund 24+ hours before, none inside) is a rule the office applies. This
  ticket should make the correct action obvious at the moment of clicking, not
  refuse the other one — a company-side cancellation is a full refund whatever
  the clock says.
