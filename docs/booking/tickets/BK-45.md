# BK-45 — The email a paying customer keeps carries none of the terms they accepted

**Status:** reviewed 2026-08-20 — 3 blockers + 3 should-fixes found and fixed, gates re-run green. Ready to commit.
**Phase:** P9 (post-deploy) · **Tier:** Reviewed — copy and customer-facing
record of a completed purchase · **Deploy:** 3. **Migration:** none.

## What the customer actually receives after paying

Found 2026-08-19 by the first real end-to-end payment on production (#35).

`markPaid()` sends **`planFirstConfirmationEmail`** (`booking-payment.ts:674`),
which is `planBoundaryEmail` — a calendar-boundary message. Its entire body is:

```
heading + lead + a 4-row table (When/Where/Service/Reference)
        + calendar line + phone line
```

**No assessment terms. No have-ready list. No visit length. No "You chose".**

## Why that contradicts what the code says about itself

`booking-email.ts` states the intent directly, in the block BK-36 edited:

> *"the confirmed message carries it because it is the one the customer keeps
> after paying, and terms that vanish from the record of a completed purchase
> are terms nobody can point at afterwards."*

That reasoning is about `customerConfirmation({ messageType: 'confirmed' })` —
which carries the terms, the have-ready list and the chosen tier. **But nothing
on the payment path sends it.** `planForAppointment` defaults `messageType` to
`'confirmed'` (`booking-admin-notify.ts:109`), so that message is what the
**"Resend confirmation"** button sends, and only that.

So there are **two materially different emails both called the confirmation**:

| | Automatic, on payment | Manual, "Resend confirmation" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builder | `planFirstConfirmationEmail` | `customerConfirmation` |
| Heading | "Your assessment is confirmed" | "You're booked" |
| Fee terms | ❌ | ✅ |
| Have-ready list | ❌ | ✅ |
| "You chose: …" | ❌ | ✅ |
| ICS | ✅ | ✅ |

The customer who pays gets the first. The customer whose office clicks Resend
gets a different, fuller message under a different heading.

**And `booking-email.ts:629-630` is stale in a way that hid this:** it says the
invite *"goes out behind the payment, from `messageType: 'confirmed'`"*. It does
not — it goes out from `planFirstConfirmationEmail`. A comment naming a path
that is not taken is how the gap stayed invisible.

## Severity — moderate, and stated honestly

It is **not** a blocker and it is **not** an information blackout:

- the customer received the full terms in the **request** email, days earlier;
- `/book/confirmed/` renders the have-ready list immediately after payment;
- the confirmation carries the operationally critical facts — time, address,
  reference, and a working ICS.

What is genuinely lost is that **the email a customer re-opens the night before
the visit** is the thinnest of the three. The have-ready list is the most
practically useful content in the whole flow and it is absent from the one
message they will actually go back to. And the record of what they paid for — the
tier, the amount, the terms — lives only in an email sent before they paid.

## The question this must answer first

**Should the two messages be merged, or should the automatic one simply grow?**
Not obvious, and worth one decision rather than a patch:

- Merging means one confirmation, one heading, one place to edit — and kills the
  "Resend sends a different email than the original" surprise the office has
  today.
- Growing `planBoundaryEmail` risks dragging fee terms into the **cancellation**
  and **restore** messages, which share that builder and must not carry them.

The second risk is the real constraint: whatever ships must not put assessment
terms into a cancellation email.

## Scope

- Decide the merge-or-grow question above and record it in the Assumptions log.
- The post-payment email carries at minimum the **have-ready list** and the
  **chosen tier and amount**; the terms are the decision above.
- Fix `booking-email.ts:629-630`'s stale comment.
- Make "Resend confirmation" send the same message the customer originally got,
  or say plainly in the UI that it does not.

## Risk areas for the reviewer

- `planBoundaryEmail` is shared by cancel / restore / first-confirmation. Any
  growth must be per-caller, not in the shared builder.
- BK-36's email assertions currently cover `customerConfirmation` for both
  message types. If the payment path changes builder, those pins must follow it
  or they will keep proving a message nobody receives.
- The ICS must not change — the calendar side of this works today.

## Planner's analysis (2026-08-20) — A RECOMMENDATION, NOT A DECISION

**THE MERGE-OR-GROW QUESTION IS STILL OPEN. The user has not answered it. Ask
before planning.** What follows is groundwork checked against the builders so
the answer can be given on facts rather than on the ticket's own summary.

### The fact that decides it: the ICS is already identical

`customerConfirmation` with `messageType: 'confirmed'` attaches
`buildBookingIcs(icsEventOf(input), 'request', input.now, icsCustomer(input.email))`
(`booking-email.ts:476`). `planBoundaryEmail` attaches
`buildBookingIcs(event, kind, now, icsCustomer(email))`
(`booking-admin-notify.ts:383`). Same METHOD, same customer attendee, same
appointment id — therefore the same UID and the same SEQUENCE story.

**"The ICS must not change" is satisfied by construction on the merge path**,
not by remembering to be careful. That was the constraint most likely to make
merging expensive and it is not there.

### The other two facts

- **`markPaid` already holds the full row.** `sendConfirmation(row, now)`
  (`booking-payment.ts:658`) takes an `Appointment`, so it has
  `assessment_tier` and every amount column. `planForAppointment(row, label,
  now)` is a drop-in — no new query, no data threaded through a route.
- **Growing is the option that carries the ticket's own stated constraint.**
  `planBoundaryEmail`'s only per-caller variation today is four copy strings
  (`booking-admin-notify.ts:390-430`). Adding body content means a fifth
  parameter passing through the **cancellation** and **restore** callers, and
  the hard rule is that assessment terms must never reach a cancellation email.
  Merging removes that risk category; growing manages it indefinitely.

### Recommendation

**Merge on the payment path**: `markPaid` sends `customerConfirmation`;
cancellation and restore keep `planBoundaryEmail` untouched, so the two
audiences stay structurally separated.

### THE RESIDUAL THIS DOES NOT FIX, and it must not be claimed as a clean collapse

**After BK-44, `update.ts` still has an inward crossing that sends
`planFirstConfirmationEmail`**: a `declined` or `payment_expired` row where the
money arrived late and stamped `paid_at` without moving the status. That path
**cannot** send `customerConfirmation` without widening `update.ts`'s snapshot —
it carries name, phone, email, address, city, postal code, service and
slot_start, and **not** the tier or the amounts (`update.ts`'s `UpdatedRow`).

So the honest scope is: *the payment path merges; the late-payment restore keeps
the thinner message.* Two builders still exist; what changes is that the one a
paying customer receives is the full one. Whether to widen the snapshot and
collapse it entirely is a separate decision and probably a separate ticket.

## Decisions (user, 2026-08-20) — the question is answered

Asked before planning, per the section above. Three answers, all client-facing:

1. **MERGE ON THE PAYMENT PATH.** `markPaid` sends the `customerConfirmation`
   message; `planBoundaryEmail` and its cancel/restore callers are not touched,
   so assessment terms remain structurally incapable of reaching a cancellation
   email. The grow option is rejected — it manages that risk indefinitely rather
   than removing it.
2. **THE AMOUNT COMES FROM THE SNAPSHOT THE CUSTOMER PAID**, itemised, falling
   back to today's tier line when the snapshot columns are null (rows predating
   BK-32). See "The defect the recommendation did not name" below — this is the
   part of the merge that is not free.
3. **THE HEADING STAYS `CONFIRMED_HEADING`** — "Your assessment is confirmed".
   The merged message does **not** inherit `customerConfirmation`'s "You're
   booked" heading, lead or subject. P9 retired the word `booked` and the email
   a paying customer keeps is the last place to reintroduce it.

## The defect the recommendation did not name, and why decision 2 exists

The recommendation to merge is right on all three of its facts — each was
re-derived against the code rather than taken from the ticket:

- `icsEventOf(input)` (`booking-email.ts:1067`) and
  `inviteEventFromAppointment(row, label)` (`booking-admin-notify.ts:223`) build
  the **same eight-field `IcsEvent`** from the same row, and both attach METHOD
  `request` with `icsCustomer(email)`. Same `now` ⇒ byte-identical attachment.
  This is assertable rather than a matter of care, and P1 below asserts it.
- `sendConfirmation(row: Appointment, now)` holds the whole row.
- growing threads a fifth parameter through `planCancellationEmail`.

**But a straight merge would put a WRONG AMOUNT into the record of a completed
purchase, which is worse than the thin email this ticket is about.**

`customerConfirmation`'s "You chose:" line calls `assessmentSummary`
(`booking-email.ts:915-925`), which calls
`assessmentQuote({ tier, service, slotStart })` — **with no `travelFeeCents`**,
so it defaults to zero (`booking-pricing.ts:188`) — and recomputes from *today's*
price table instead of reading BK-32's snapshot columns
(`assessment_amount_cents`, `travel_fee_cents`, `gst_cents`,
`total_amount_cents`, `paid_amount_cents`).

**That is structurally the same defect as BK-46's header total, arriving in the
email.** On the *request* message it is correct — it is a quote, before
approval, and no snapshot exists yet. On the *post-payment* message it is a
receipt-shaped claim about money already taken, and it is wrong on every booking
where the office adjusted the amount or added travel. The user confirmed
2026-08-20 that travel is the figure the office adjusts most often.

So the merge carries one real piece of work: **the confirmed arm must render
what the row says was charged, not what the pricing table says it would cost
today.**

## Plan

### T1 — the settled amounts reach the message

`BookingNotificationInput` gains one **required** field (not optional):

```ts
/** BK-45. What the office actually charged, from the row. Null before approval. */
settled: SettledAmounts | null;

export type SettledAmounts = {
  baseCents: number;
  travelCents: number;
  gstCents: number;
  totalCents: number;
  /** What actually arrived (`paid_amount_cents`). Null when nothing has. */
  paidCents: number | null;
};
```

**Required rather than optional, deliberately.** An optional field is how a
future construction site silently omits the amounts and ships a confirmation
with none; a required one makes typecheck name every site. Seven sites exist:
`planForPayload`, `planForAppointment`, `api/booking/create.ts:216`,
`cron/expire-payments.ts:350`, `review.ts:124`, and the `INSURANCE` / `PRIVATE`
fixtures in `verify-booking-email.ts`. The shape mirrors `ApprovalDetails`'
first four fields on purpose — the customer sees the same itemisation twice,
once when asked to pay and once when confirmed.

- `planForAppointment` populates it from the row when `total_amount_cents` is
  non-null, and passes `paidCents: row.paid_amount_cents`.
- `planForPayload` passes `null` — at submission there is no snapshot, and a
  quote is the honest thing for a request message to carry.
- Every other site passes `null` (none of them builds a paid confirmation).

### T2 — the confirmed arm renders it

In `customerConfirmation`, on the `!isRequest` branch only:

- when `input.settled` is non-null, render an **Amount** block in the same row
  shape `approvalMessage` uses (`Assessment` / `Travel` **only when > 0** /
  `GST` / `Total`), and the tier line becomes the tier NAME alone —
  `You chose: Standard assessment` — with no recomputed figure beside it;
- when `input.settled` is null, today's `assessmentSummary` line renders
  unchanged, so nothing regresses for a row with no snapshot;
- the request arm is untouched in every case.

**`paidCents` is not asserted to equal `totalCents`.** A partial or over-payment
is a real state (`markPaid` records what arrived and flags the row), so the
block states the itemised charge, and names what arrived on its own line **only
when it differs from the total**. The email never claims the total was paid
when the row says otherwise.

### T3 — the heading (decision 3)

The `!isRequest` arm's heading, lead and subject move to `CONFIRMED_HEADING` /
`CONFIRMED_LEAD` / a subject built from `CONFIRMED_HEADING`. Both the payment
path and Resend read this one builder, so **they change together and cannot
diverge**. `You're booked` disappears from `booking-email.ts` entirely.

Two comments are **falsified by this change** and are therefore in scope:
`resend.ts:22` and `[id].astro:168` both describe the button as re-sending a
"You're booked" message. Rewritten to name what it now sends. (`EMAIL_MESSAGES.
refused`'s *"only sent for a booked appointment"* is a **pre-existing**
office-facing claim this change does not falsify — it was already wrong, the
status is `confirmed`. Recorded to ROADMAP Known traps under BK-47, not fixed
here.)

### T4 — `markPaid` sends the merged message

`booking-payment.ts`'s `sendConfirmation`: the customer send builds
`planForAppointment(row, SERVICE_LABELS[row.service] ?? row.service, now)
.customer` instead of `planFirstConfirmationEmail(event, email, now)`.

**Three things deliberately do NOT change**, each for a stated reason:

- **the sender stays `sendCalendarInvite`** with `{ id, kind: 'request', now,
  audience: 'customer' }`. The message is still ICS-bearing, and keeping it
  preserves the idempotency prefix (`booking-<id>-request-<seq>`) and the mute
  line that `verify-booking-admin-db.ts` arms read. Switching to
  `sendCustomerConfirmation` would change both for no gain.
- **nothing stamps `confirmation_sent_at`.** `sendConfirmationAndStamp` writes
  it; `markPaid` does not, and must not start — that column currently records
  the *request* acknowledgement, and stamping it here would overwrite that fact.
  The column's mislabelling is BK-46's, and this ticket must not half-move it.
- **the office invite is untouched.**

The null-email case behaves exactly as today: `plan.customer` is null when the
row has no address, and no customer send is queued.

### T5 — `booking-email.ts:629-630`'s stale comment

The old sentence — *"the invite goes out behind the payment, from
`messageType: 'confirmed'`"* — becomes **true** after T4, which is precisely why
it must still be rewritten rather than left standing. It is true and
**incomplete**, and an incomplete claim is what `update.ts:266` was. The
replacement names both paths: `markPaid` sends
`planForAppointment(...).customer`, and `update.ts`'s late-payment inward
crossing still sends `planFirstConfirmationEmail`.

### T6 — the pins (the trap this ticket names itself)

**P1 — THE RELATIONSHIP PIN, and it is the load-bearing one.** For one paid
fixture row and one `now`, the customer message the **payment** path builds and
the customer message the **resend** path builds are compared field by field —
`subject`, `html`, `text`, and the ICS attachment **content**, not just its
presence. Not "each carries the terms" twice; one assertion that they are the
same message. This is BK-44's invariant lesson applied: the door no per-message
assertion names is one path being repointed later.

**What P1 cannot catch, stated because BK-44's N1 required it:** both sides
derive from `planForAppointment`, so a change that moves BOTH keeps it green.
P2 is what catches one of them being repointed.

**P2 — the source pins.** `booking-payment.ts`'s customer send names
`planForAppointment` and **no other** customer-message builder; `resend.ts`
likewise; and `update.ts` **still** names `planFirstConfirmationEmail`, so the
residual is a pinned decision rather than drift somebody later "tidies".

**P3 — content, on the message that is now actually sent.** BK-36's terms pins
already drive `planBookingNotifications({...INSURANCE, messageType})` for both
types, so after T4 they cover the payment path for the first time — but nothing
*says* so, which is exactly the trap. Added: the confirmed message carries
`HAVE_READY_ITEMS`, the tier name, and the settled total; and **the rendered
total equals `total_amount_cents` rather than `assessmentQuote`'s
recomputation** — driven by a fixture whose travel fee is non-zero and whose
snapshot disagrees with today's price table, which is the only shape that can
tell the two apart.

**P4 — the negative pin the rejected option would have needed.** No message
built by `planBoundaryEmail` — cancellation, restore, first-confirmation —
carries the fee terms, the have-ready list or an amount. This is the constraint
that decided the merge, so it is asserted rather than trusted to the fact that
nobody edited that builder.

**P5 — the claim grep, not the constant.** `You're booked` and the
`BOOKED_CLAIM_SHAPES` grammars are absent from every render of the confirmed
customer message. Pinned by claim shape, per CLAUDE.md's copy-inventory trap.

The shape of the review is worth recording: **the plan applied CLAUDE.md's

### B1 (blocker) — the merged message would tell a customer who had just paid that we were about to email them a payment link · **FIXED, decision 4**

`booking-email.ts:381-390` renders the fee terms unconditionally on both arms,
and three of those constants are written in pre-payment future tense:

- `FEE_TERMS_INTRO` (`booking-copy.ts:233`) — *"Once we approve yours, we email
  you a secure payment link — and your appointment is confirmed as soon as the
  payment goes through."*
- `FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT[2]` (`:328`) — *"Nothing is charged when you send your
  request…"*
- `FEE_TERMS_PAYMENT[3]` (`:329`) — *"If we do not have the payment in time, we
  may release the time, and you are welcome to book again."*

Under `CONFIRMED_HEADING`, seconds after `markPaid` recorded the money. **The
