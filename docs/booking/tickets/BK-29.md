# BK-29 — Remove the "free assessment" claim site-wide

**Status:** implemented 2026-08-15. Light tier, no reviews by design. Gates
green, 7 red rows observed. Sweep complete: **0 claims reach the browser**.
See *Implementation notes* at the foot.
**Phase:** P8 · **Tier:** Light (copy) — see *Tier* below, it is not a
comfortable fit · **Depends on:** BK-27 (shares `book.astro`; its constants are
the only place a price may live) · **Blocks:** **BK-27's deploy.** Hard blocker,
not a follow-up.

## Why

The client's 2026-08-14 pricing model made "free assessment" **false**, not
conditional. Every customer now pays at the visit ($399 / $699 / $1,199 + GST)
and the fee is credited against the final invoice only if they go ahead. Nobody
gets a free assessment at the point of sale.

Under the superseded waiver model the claim was defensible — free for the
customers who proceeded, which is most of them — and BK-27 accordingly reworded
only the two booking surfaces and parked the rest as a Known trap. **That
argument is gone.** What is left is ~51 live claims that are simply untrue.

Two things make this a blocker on BK-27 rather than a tidy-up:

1. **They contradict each other on the same screen.** `<Navbar />` renders "Free
   Assessment" into `/book/` itself — `dist/client/book/index.html` contains the
   phrase **7 times** today, `dist/client/index.html` **12**, `/contact/` **7**.
   Shipping BK-27 alone puts a nav pill saying "Free Assessment" in the same
   viewport as a box quoting $399 + GST.
2. **Canada's Competition Bureau** requires ads using "free" to *clearly disclose
   all conditions*, and the test weighs "both the literal meaning of the
   information and the general impression it makes". A page whose nav, title and
   CTA all say free fails the general-impression half however good the terms box
   below it is.

### The worst single instance

`src/pages/book/confirmed.astro:8` — *"Your free on-site restoration assessment
is booked."* That is the page a customer lands on **immediately after ticking a
box agreeing to pay**. It contradicts the acknowledgment BK-27 just captured, on
the very next screen, in the one place a dispute would start. It is a different
class from a marketing headline and should be fixed first even if the ticket is
split.

`/contact/` is the second-worst: `contact.astro:71` and `ContactForm.svelte:127`
both promise a "free on-site assessment", and `/contact/` is the one surface
BK-27 deliberately keeps the fee terms *off*. A visitor there gets an
unqualified free claim with no terms available anywhere on the page.

## Tier

The ROADMAP filed this as **Light** and by CLAUDE.md's letter that is right: it
is copy. No write path, no PII, no concurrency, nothing irreversible.

It is worth saying plainly that the letter and the spirit diverge here. Light
means "gates only, no reviews", and this ticket changes what the business claims
in ~51 customer-facing places including structured data, meta descriptions and
`llms.txt` — the surface an AI assistant quotes. A careless sweep can break JSON-LD
or leave a half-qualified claim that reads worse than the original.

**Recommendation: keep Light, and put the weight on the gate instead of a
review** — it is designed and red-first'd to Reviewed standards, because the
gate is what stops the claim coming back. If the user wants a review anyway,
**the thing worth reviewing is the replacement copy, not the diff.**

*(The draft also recommended holding the wording for client sign-off before
shipping. **Reversed on implementation** — see "The client-sign-off carve-out
was dropped" in the implementation notes. Nothing here invents a business fact,
so the standing "implementer drafts, client edits later" rule applies, and a
held-back batch would have meant committing a permanently red gate.)*

## Design decisions

- **THE FIGURES DO NOT LEAVE `booking-copy.ts`.** The single strongest
  constraint on this ticket. BK-27's whole design puts the prices in one module
  so a client change lands once; scattering "$399" across 22 marketing files
  would recreate the (780) 244-4747 trap 51 times over and guarantee a stale
  price within a quarter. **No page outside the booking surfaces states a
  price.**
- **Therefore: remove the claim, do not replace it with the price.** The
  marketing surfaces lose "free" and say nothing about cost. Only `/book/` and
  the homepage booking section — which already render the terms from the
  constants — talk about money at all.
- **Removal is not deletion.** "Free assessment" is doing real work in a CTA;
  cutting the word and leaving "Assessment" makes weaker copy than the site had.
  Each class gets a replacement that is true and still sells:
  - **CTAs and labels** → "Book Your Assessment" (BK-27's precedent, already
    live on the two booking surfaces).
  - **Meta descriptions / SEO** → swap the benefit for one that is true and
    already claimed elsewhere: 24/7 response, ~60-minute arrival, IICRC
    certification, direct insurance billing, written guarantee.
  - **FAQ answers** ("we give you a straight answer at the free assessment") →
    drop the adjective; the sentence works unchanged.
  - **`services.ts:260` `timing: 'Free assessment'`** → a process-step label;
    "Assessment" or "Day one".
  - **`llms.txt`** → must be accurate, not merely inoffensive; an assistant
    quoting it is a customer-facing statement with no page around it to qualify
    the claim. State the assessment is paid and credited, without the figures,
    and link to `/book/`.
  - **Blog posts** → body copy; drop the adjective.
- **`confirmed.astro` and `/contact/` go first** and could ship alone if this
  ticket has to be split.
- **Out of scope:** anything that would restate the pricing model outside the
  booking surfaces, and the `workflow-options.md` decision.

## Verification (gates + red-first)

**The gate must read the BUILT output, not source.** This is the ticket's one
real design decision and it comes from BK-27's re-review: `/book/` renders the
phrase 7 times without `book.astro` containing it once, because `<Navbar />`
does. A source-level grep over the page files would pass while the page still
says it.

`verify-cutover.ts` is the home — it already runs a build and greps `dist/` for
the sentinel labels, and it already owns source-level surface claims. Extend it:

- After the build, assert **no page under `dist/client/**/*.html` contains
  "free assessment"**, entity-normalised (`&nbsp;` renders identically), matched
  case-insensitively with flexible whitespace.
- Report **every** offending file on failure, not the first — a sweep of 51
  claims will be iterated on, and a gate that names one file per run turns a
  morning into a day.
- Keep BK-27's two source-level pins as they are. They are narrower and they
  fail earlier with a better message; this one is the backstop that catches
  what source cannot see.
- `llms.txt` and `sitemap`/JSON-LD are generated too — assert `llms.txt`
  explicitly, since it is not an `.html` file and the glob above would miss it.

## Open questions for the client (log answers in Assumptions)

1. **Sign-off on the replacement wording**, same carve-out as BK-27's pricing.
   This is ~51 customer-facing claims and the marketing copy is the client's, not
   the implementer's.
2. **Does the site say the assessment is paid at all, outside `/book/`?** The
   design decision above says no — marketing surfaces go silent on cost, and only
   the booking surfaces carry the terms. The alternative is a one-line qualifier
   ("assessment fee credited to your invoice") on service pages. **Recommendation:
   stay silent.** A visitor who is ready to book reaches a page that tells them in
   full; a visitor reading a service page is not choosing on price, and every
   extra surface that mentions money is another one to update when the model
   changes again — which it has done twice in two days.
3. **Ads and GSC.** "Free assessment" may appear in Google Ads copy and will
   appear in indexed titles/descriptions for weeks after deploy. The site is the
   part we control; the ad account is the user's. Worth a checklist item, and it
   is the reason the `<title>` change lands in this commit rather than being
   deferred again.

## Implementation notes (2026-08-15)

Swept in one pass. The gate was written **first**, watched name all 18 offending
built files, then the sweep ran until it was green — the sequence the
*Verification* section calls for.

### The client-sign-off carve-out was dropped, deliberately

The draft said the replacement wording needed client sign-off before it could
ship, "same carve-out as BK-27's pricing". **That was wrong and it was reversed
on implementation.** BK-27's carve-out existed because inventing a *price*
fabricates a business fact. Nothing here is invented: every replacement draws on
a claim the site already makes elsewhere — IICRC certification, 24/7 response,
~60-minute arrival, direct insurance billing, written scope, written guarantee.
The repo's standing rule therefore applies (`booking-copy.ts` header: *the
implementer drafts, the client edits later*).

It also could not have worked. The gate goes red until the **last** claim is
gone, so a batch held for the client would have meant committing a red gate or
not committing the gate at all.

### Replacement pattern actually used

- **CTAs / buttons / eyebrows** → "Book an Assessment", "Book Your Assessment",
  "Book Assessment" (length-matched to the slot; BK-27's precedent).
- **The `free` benefit in prose and SEO strings** → "written scope", which is
  what the assessment actually produces and is already claimed site-wide.
- **FAQ answers** → adjective dropped; sentences otherwise untouched, so the
  rendered FAQ and the FAQPage JSON-LD that reads the same strings stay in sync.
- **`services.ts:260` `timing: 'Free assessment'`** → `'Assessment'`.
- **`llms.txt`** → states the mechanism in words, no figures: the assessment is
  paid at the end of the visit and credited in full against the final invoice if
  the customer goes ahead. An assistant quoting it now says something true.
- **`book.astro`'s `<title>` and schema `name`** → "Book an Assessment". BK-27
  deferred these to keep the search-surface change in one commit; this is that
  commit.

### Two things found mid-sweep

- **`insurance-claims.astro:310` also said "no obligation"** — the same
  falsified claim in different words, which BK-27's pin only covers on two
  files. Removed here rather than recorded as an out-of-scope defect: it is the
  same claim, on a page this ticket was already editing, and leaving it would
  have meant the sweep knowingly shipped a false statement.
- **`404.html` and `blog/index.html`** carried the claim transitively via
  `Layout`/`BlogLayout` and were in no source inventory. Only the built-output
  gate saw them.

### The gate's own defect, found by its red pass

Row (f) is the one worth reading. The first version of the pattern was
`/free(\s|&nbsp;)+assessments?/i` — which reads like a check on "does this page
say the assessment is free" and is really a check on whether two words are
neighbours. `/book/confirmed/` says *"Your **free on-site restoration**
assessment is booked"*, three words in between, and **stayed green**. It had
been listed in the very first red run only because the Navbar's "Free
Assessment" was still on that page — so the file looked covered while the claim
actually on it was invisible.

Now two shapes, for the claim's two grammars: adjectival (`free … assessment`)
and predicative (`assessment … is free`), each bounded by `[^.<>]` so a match
cannot cross a sentence break or leave its element.

**This is the fifth time in P8 that a content assertion was written against the
sentence in front of the author instead of the claim being made** — the credit
regex (too narrow), the refund regex (too loose), the payment-timing regex (too
loose), and now this one twice over. The pattern is stable enough to name: *when
a gate asserts a claim rather than a token, enumerate the claim's grammars and
bound the match; then break it by REPHRASING, never by deleting the keyword.* A
delete-the-keyword red passes for every one of these bugs.
