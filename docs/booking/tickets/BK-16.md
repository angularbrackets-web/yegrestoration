**The scope is two halves, and the second forces the first.** A CANCEL only
clears an event the customer's calendar already holds under the same UID —
and today the customer confirmation is deliberately attachment-free (BK-14
out-of-scope, recorded as "the natural follow-up if he asks"; he has). So:

1. Every customer confirmation email gains the METHOD:REQUEST invite (all
   three senders of it: public booking, admin entry with the checkbox, the
   resend button).
2. A status edit crossing the cancelled boundary in **either direction**
   mails the customer (when the row has an email) alongside the office
   invite BK-14 already sends: into `cancelled`, a cancellation email
   carrying METHOD:CANCEL — same UID, later SEQUENCE; out of `cancelled`, a
   restore email carrying a fresh METHOD:REQUEST — same UID, later still.
   Both directions in one ticket because of the plan-review blocker (Status
   above): the restore cannot ride the resend button, whose fixed
   idempotency key Resend would collapse into the original confirmation.

Cancellation remains phone-in (locked): this email *confirms* a cancellation
the customer almost always asked for by phone; it is not a self-service
cancel path and carries no cancel link.

Risk areas, in review-weight order:

1. **A deliberate assertion flip.** "The customer message carries no
   attachment" is asserted today in `verify-booking-email.ts`,
   `verify-booking-admin-entry.ts`, and `verify-booking-admin-db.ts` — BK-14
   pinned the *absence*. This ticket inverts those asserts to "exactly one,
   REQUEST-typed, attendee = the customer" — each flip is a weakening risk
   if done sloppily (an assert deleted instead of inverted stays green
   forever). Every flipped assert is red-observed in its new direction.
2. **PII in two new customer artifacts.** The cancellation email is a
   customer message: it must not read `policyNumber`/`claimNumber`/
   `insurerName` at all (the `customerConfirmation` precedent). The customer
   ICS is bound by the ICS rule already — `IcsEvent` structurally cannot
   carry them — but the *audience* wiring is new and gets its own sentinel
   red.
3. **Lifecycle correctness across two calendars.** The customer CANCEL must
   carry the same UID as the confirmation's REQUEST and a strictly greater
   SEQUENCE, and ORGANIZER byte-identical — Gmail/Outlook matching, same
   facts as BK-14 risk area 4. The office and customer artifacts share the
   UID on purpose: they are one event.
4. **The update route's posture.** The new customer sends on the boundary
   must stay best-effort (never fail the redirect), run concurrently with
   the office invite under ONE `POST_COMMIT_BUDGET_MS` window (the admin
   entry's `Promise.all` precedent), and send nothing on re-submits,
   `completed`/`no_show` edits that don't cross the boundary, and the
   23505 arm.

## Mechanism — building on BK-14, pinned there, not re-derived

- **Audience parameter on the builder — REQUIRED, never defaulted**
  (plan-review S2). `buildBookingIcs(event, kind, now)` hard-codes
  `ATTENDEE:...:mailto:${BOOKING_INTERNAL_TO}` and a DESCRIPTION carrying
  the *customer's* phone (office-facing: "who do I call to reach the
  customer"). It gains an explicit audience argument — `{ attendee: string;
  contactLine: string }` or equivalent — office callers pass the office
  address + customer phone line exactly as today (byte-identical output for
  the office audience is an acceptance criterion, pinned per S1 below);
  customer callers pass the customer's email and a reschedule line built
  from `SUPPORT_PHONE` (their own phone number in their own calendar event
  helps nobody). A *defaulted* office audience would let a customer-path
  caller that forgets the argument silently mail the customer an
  office-attendee ICS with typecheck green — so the parameter is required
  at every call site, and the ripple is priced in: every existing
  `buildBookingIcs` call (`booking-email.ts`, `planCalendarInvite`, and
  ~20 sites in `verify-booking-ics.ts`) is edited to say `office`
  explicitly. `UID`, `ORGANIZER`, `SEQUENCE`, `DTSTART/DTEND`, folding,
  escaping: unchanged and shared.
- **The byte-identical AC has a mechanism: a golden pin** (plan-review S1).
  After the refactor there is no "old builder" to compare against, so
  `verify-booking-ics.ts` records the exact pre-refactor office-audience
  ICS text for one fixture event (deterministic — injected `now`, fixed
  fields) as a literal, and asserts the refactored office-audience call
  reproduces it byte-for-byte. Red by perturbing the office contact line.
  Without this the AC is decoration; a self-referential comparison cannot
  fail.
- **The mute log line becomes audience-attributable** (plan-review S3).
  `sendCalendarInvite`'s mute line names only the booking id, so
  "cancel-with-email produces two sends" would count two identical lines —
  red for a never-wired customer send, but green for a route that sends the
  office invite twice. The line gains the kind and audience
  (`no calendar request/cancel (office|customer) for booking <id>`); the
  existing `muteLine` regexes in `verify-booking-admin-db.ts` tolerate a
  suffix after the id, and the route arms assert the two lines are
  *distinguishable*, not merely two.
- **The resend button's fixed key, stated so nobody re-designates it.**
  `sendCustomerConfirmation` keys on `booking-<id>` — byte-identical
  between the booking-time confirmation and any later resend, so within
  Resend's dedupe window a resend is silently collapsed. Pre-BK-16 that was
  a low-stakes suppressed duplicate; it is why the resend button CANNOT be
  the calendar-restore path (the blocker), and it stays out of this
  ticket's scope to change — recorded here so the next ticket that wants
  to lean on resend re-reads this paragraph.
- **METHOD:REQUEST for the customer invite, not PUBLISH.** REQUEST with the
  customer as ATTENDEE is what Calendly-class products send, it is what
  BK-14 proved renders in Gmail, and CANCEL-matching against it is the
  tested path. The RSVP a client may offer replies to the noreply
  organizer; harmless, accepted.
- **Idempotency**: the confirmation (now carrying an attachment) keeps its
  existing per-booking prefix — same message class, retry semantics
  unchanged. The customer cancellation email is a *transition* send and
  uses the BK-14 per-transition prefix via `sendCalendarInvite`, whose
  key is `booking-<id>-cancel-<seq>:<to>` — the customer's `to` differs
  from the office's, so the two concurrent CANCEL sends cannot collapse
  into each other.
- **The self-join already carries almost everything.** `update.ts`'s
  snapshot returns id, name, phone, address, city, postal_code, service,
  slot_start. It gains `email` (outside the SET whitelist, so snapshot =
  post-update value). No second SELECT — the route's docstring still
  forbids it.
- **`slotLabel`** for the cancellation copy comes from `formatSlot`
  (the one zone converter), called where the plan is built — the
  `planForPayload` precedent.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A public booking's customer confirmation carries exactly one `.ics`:
   METHOD:REQUEST, UID `booking-<id>@yegrestoration.ca`, ATTENDEE the
   customer's mailto, ORGANIZER unchanged, no policy/claim/insurer anywhere
   in message or ICS (fully-insured fixture). Admin entry with the checkbox
   and the resend button produce the same attachment.
2. The office internal notification's ICS is byte-identical to BK-14's
   output for the same input.
3. A status edit into `cancelled` on a row with an email sends exactly two
   mails: the office CANCEL and the customer cancellation email whose ICS is
   METHOD:CANCEL + STATUS:CANCELLED, same UID as AC1's, SEQUENCE strictly
   greater than the confirmation's. Un-cancel sends the office REQUEST and
   the customer restore email (fresh REQUEST, same UID, greater still).
   Without an email: office invite only, in both directions.
4. Re-submits, non-boundary completed/no_show edits, and the 23505 arm send
   the customer nothing; the narrowed office invariant is untouched. The
   office-audience ICS is byte-identical to BK-14's output (the golden
   pin).
5. All new sends are best-effort: with sends failing or the key unset,
   the status edit still redirects `?saved=1` and failures are logged.
6. Gates green: typecheck, build, `verify:booking:ics`,
   `verify:booking:email`, `verify:booking:admin:entry`,
   `verify:booking:admin:db`, regressions `verify:contact`,
   `verify:booking:smoke`, `verify:cutover`. Red-first per failure mode,
   including every flipped assertion seen red in its new direction.
7. Post-deploy (user): book a test with a real inbox → confirmation renders
   an invite → add it → cancel in admin → cancellation email arrives and
   the customer-side event clears → un-cancel in admin → restore email
   arrives and the event returns → delete the test row.

## Assumptions log

- **The confirmation gains the invite in the same ticket** — the CANCEL is
  inert without it, and BK-14 recorded exactly this follow-up.
- **Un-cancel auto-mails the customer a restore** — forced by the
  plan-review blocker (the resend button's key collapses), and symmetric
  with the cancellation the client approved. The client approved
  "cancellation email"; the restore direction is the reviewer-forced
  completion and is **the client's to overturn** — without it, an
  un-cancelled customer's calendar stays showing "cancelled" with no
  working recovery.
- **REQUEST, not PUBLISH**, for the customer ICS — Mechanism.
- **No URL in either boundary email** — consistent with the confirmation's
  asserted no-URL rule; rebooking is by phone or by the site the customer
  already knows.
- **`completed → cancelled` also mails the customer "cancelled"**
  (plan-review N4) — the boundary rule inherited from BK-14 is
  direction-of-crossing, not status-pair; consistent, and recorded as a
  decision rather than discovered later.
- **Pre-BK-16 rows hold no invite**, so a cancellation mailed to one
  carries a CANCEL for an event their calendar never held (plan-review
  N1). Inert — calendar clients ignore a CANCEL for an unknown UID — and
  the email body is still the written confirmation the feature is for.
- **Office ICS byte-stability is an AC**, so BK-14's Gmail-proven behaviour
  cannot drift as a side effect of the audience refactor.

### Settled during implementation

- **The audience is a discriminated union, not `{ attendee, contactLine }`.**
  The Mechanism says "or equivalent", and the loose-pair form has a spelling
  the union does not: the office address with the customer's contact line, or
  a customer attendee with the customer's own phone printed back at them.
  `{ audience: 'office' } | { audience: 'customer'; email }` makes the two
  audience-shaped facts move together and makes the customer case
  unconstructible without the address the ATTENDEE needs. `ICS_OFFICE` is a
  const and `icsCustomer(email)` a one-line constructor, so the ~20 call sites
  still say `office` explicitly, which is what the plan review asked for.
- **`CANCEL_LINE` is the customer's contact line**, not a new string. It is
  already the settled "To cancel or reschedule, call or text
  ${SUPPORT_PHONE}." in `booking-copy.ts` — writing a second sentence saying
  the same thing is exactly the copy-drift the phone-number trap in the
  ROADMAP is about.
- **SUMMARY and LOCATION stay audience-independent.** The ticket names only
  the attendee and the contact line as audience-shaped. A customer-flavoured
  SUMMARY ("YEG Restoration assessment") is arguably nicer, but it is scope
  the ticket did not ask for and it would put a second thing between the two
  copies of one event. Asserted equal across audiences rather than left
  unstated.
- **`sendCalendarInvite`'s `keyParts` gains `audience`, not the idempotency
  prefix.** The plan-review S3 mute line needs the audience; the *key* does
  not, because `createResendSender` already appends `:<to>` and the two
  addresses differ. Putting it in the prefix as well would imply the `to`
  suffix could not be relied on. Both facts are now stated in
  `inviteIdempotencyPrefix`'s docstring.
- **`sendBoundaryInvite` is renamed `sendBoundaryMail`.** It no longer sends
  only invites. The source pin that counts it was updated in the same change;
  a rename that left the pin naming the old symbol would have gone red, which
  is the pin working.
- **The deadline's timeout value is one entry per attempted send.** The office
  send always runs, the customer send only sometimes, so the fallback array is
  built from `sends.length` rather than hard-coded to two — a hard-coded pair
  would log a phantom customer failure on every no-email cancellation.
- **The blank-email arm writes `email = '   '` with SQL.** `parseAdminEntry`
  normalizes a blank to NULL, so the trim half of the guard (plan-review N2)
  could not be reached through a route — and a NULL column already fails
  `if (email)` without it. Found by the red pass: the trim was untested until
  this arm existed. See the red table's entry for it.
- **BK-14's ticket keeps its historical record.** Scope 6 says the
  "customer-facing ICS deferred" note gains "→ done in BK-16"; that note lives
  in `BK-14.md`'s Out of scope, not in the ROADMAP. A one-line forward pointer
  was added there, attributed to this ticket, and nothing else in that
  committed ticket was touched — the same call BK-14 itself made about BK-08's
  invariant wording.
- **Out of scope, recorded not fixed: BK-20 is cancelled.** The client
  reverted it on 2026-08-12 (told to the implementer during this ticket) and
  Fridays stay completely closed. The ROADMAP's P6 row and paragraph, which
  had been written as an amendment to the locked Friday rule, now say so —
  including that the Locked section's "except Friday" stands unchanged. No
  code was touched: `CLOSED_WEEKDAYS = [5]` was never edited, because BK-20
  had not started.

### ⚠ One assertion was green against a real break, and is recorded rather than quietly fixed

`verify-booking-admin-db.ts`'s "sent the customer nothing — there is no address
on the row" **stayed GREEN** the first time the guard was broken. The break was
`const email = row.email ?? ''`, and `''` is falsy, so `if (email)` still
skipped — the guard was never actually defeated and the assertion could not
have failed. Two things came out of it, both above:

1. The guard has **two** halves and a NULL column only exercises one. `if
   (email)` alone is already false for NULL, so the `.trim() !== ''` the
   resend route contributed (plan-review N2) was pinned by nothing at all.
   A blank-email arm now writes `email = '   '` straight to the column —
   `parseAdminEntry` normalizes a blank to NULL and cannot produce that row,
   which is precisely why some other writer could.
2. The no-address assertion is now red-observable by the break that genuinely
   defeats the guard: removing it and sending unconditionally.

This is the same family as BK-14's three red-pass findings and BK-09's
`size_bytes` note — an assertion that reads correct and cannot fail. It is
local to this ticket's guard rather than a technique the repo reuses, so it is
recorded here rather than promoted to the ROADMAP's Known traps.
