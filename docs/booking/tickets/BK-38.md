The day strip called every empty day **Full**. Two of the three reasons a day
is empty are not demand:

- **Fridays are closed by configuration** (`CLOSED_WEEKDAYS = [5]`, Locked).
  The caption directly under the strip already says so in prose — "We're closed
  Fridays" — so the widget was contradicting itself on screen, and a visitor
  reading "Full" on both Fridays for two weeks concludes the company is booked
  solid rather than closed.
- **Today goes empty at lunchtime** because of `MIN_NOTICE_HOURS`, not because
  anyone booked it. Found while checking this ticket against the live
  availability endpoint: at the time of writing, `2026-08-16` came back with
  zero slots and rendered "Full" (see Assumptions log, item 3).

Plus the strip carried no month at all, which is the ambiguity the client hit:
15 chips of bare day numbers, and one of them may be in the next month.

## Scope

1. `dayChipKind(date, weekday, slotCount, now)` in `booking-form.ts` — pure,
   four states: `open` | `closed` | `elapsed` | `full`.
2. `dayChipCaption` — `N open` | `Closed` | `Call us` | `Full`.
3. `dayChipLabel(date, weekday, showMonth)` and `startsNewMonth` — a boundary
   chip reads `Sep 1` instead of `Tue 1`. No year.
4. `monthHeading(date)` and a header above the strip that follows the scroll.
5. The island passes ONE clock (`availabilityAt`, stamped when availability
   lands) rather than reading `new Date()` per chip.

## Out of scope, deliberately

- **Blackout dates keep saying "Full".** Rendering them as "Closed" would need
  the availability endpoint to disclose *why* a day is empty, which
  `booking-availability.ts:48-53` refuses to do by design ("distinguishing
  'blacked out' from 'already booked' leaks the schedule"). Overturning that is
  a client-facing disclosure decision, not an implementer's. **User decision
  2026-08-16: Fridays only.** Nothing in this ticket asks the server a new
  question — `closed` comes from a public constant and `elapsed` from public
  constants plus the visitor's own clock.
- The picker redesign. That is BK-18, still deferred behind P7/P9.

## Assumptions log

1. **The month goes on the boundary chip only, not on every chip of the new
   month.** The header above the strip carries the month continuously; putting
   it on every chip would make the boundary chip — the one genuinely ambiguous
   position in the row — look no different from its neighbours.
2. **A boundary chip loses its weekday** (`Sep 1`, not `Tue Sep 1`). User's
   stated preference, and the header plus the neighbouring chips supply the
   context. Recorded because it is a real trade, not an oversight: exactly one
   chip per window shows no weekday.
3. **`elapsed` was added to this ticket rather than recorded as an
   out-of-scope defect.** CLAUDE.md says a defect found mid-ticket goes to
   Known traps, never fixed inline. Judged in scope instead, because the
   ticket's own statement of the problem is "closed days must be distinct from
   days where all slots are **genuinely taken**", and a day emptied by the
   notice rule is not taken. It is provable client-side with no disclosure —
   every one of the day's five grid instants is earlier than
   `earliestBookableInstant(now)`, so no booking is involved in it being empty.
   **Flagged to the user in the same message as the result; strip it if the
   call goes the other way** (delete the `elapsed` arm, its caption, and the
   three assertions naming it).

### Red-observed

| # | Break | Assertion that went red |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | `dayChipKind` returns `'full'` for a closed weekday | a zero-slot closed weekday is Closed, not Full |
| b | weekday tested before slot count | a closed weekday that the server offered slots on is still selectable |
| c | `dayChipLabel` ignores `showMonth` | a boundary chip carries the month |
| d | `monthHeading` drops the `- 1` | the header names month and year; December is not off by one |
| e | `startsNewMonth` returns true for the first chip | the first chip in the strip is not a boundary |
| f | `dayChipCaption` swaps Closed and Full | the closed caption reads Closed; the full caption reads Full |
| g | the `elapsed` arm deleted | a day whose every slot is past the notice cutoff is not Full |
| h | `.every(` → `.some(` on the elapsed test | a PARTLY elapsed day that is empty is Full |
| i | `closed` / `elapsed` precedence swapped | a closed weekday that has also elapsed reads Closed |
| j | the `Call us` caption falls back to `Full` | the elapsed caption hands over to the phone |

**Row (h) is the one worth reading.** It stayed GREEN on the first attempt.
The assertion set at that point had a clock 12 hours before the first slot (no
slot elapsed → `some` and `every` agree) and a clock after the last (all
elapsed → they agree again); neither separates the two quantifiers. The break
was left in place and the ASSERTION was fixed, not the code: a third clock is
now derived as `gridToday[1] - MIN_NOTICE_HOURS`, which puts the cutoff exactly
on the second slot so the first is past it and the rest are not. `some` then
claims "Call us" on a day that still had bookable time and was empty because it
was booked — a lie about demand in the opposite direction to the one this
ticket fixes. This is the "assertion that cannot fail" family the ROADMAP keeps
paying for; it is now five deep.
