# BK-48 — The GST registration number is on no receipt we issue

**Status:** committed 2026-08-20 on `main` — DEPLOYED
**Phase:** P9 (post-deploy) · **Tier:** Reviewed — it is a tax-compliance
statement on the record of a completed purchase · **Deploy:** immediate.
**Migration:** none.

Found 2026-08-20 by the receipt from live booking #36 — the first real charge
this business has ever taken.

## What the customer receives

The Stripe receipt for a $628.43 charge reads:

```
On-site restoration assessment × 1     CA$598.50
GST (5%) × 1                            CA$29.93
Amount paid                            CA$628.43
```

**No registration number anywhere**, and the payment page states
*"No tax rate applied."* Our own approval and confirmation emails itemise the
GST amount and print no number either. So a customer who paid GST has nothing
from us that lets them claim it.

## Why the Dashboard step did not fix it

The number was added to Stripe on 2026-08-20 under Settings → Billing →
**Invoices** → "Invoice tax information". That field governs **invoices**. A
Checkout Session issues a **receipt**, which is a different artifact, and our
GST is a hand-built line item rather than a Stripe Tax rate — so Stripe has no
tax registration associated with the charge and renders none.

**Two claims in ROADMAP's Known traps were wrong and are corrected there:** that
Stripe renders tax info on Checkout receipts from Dashboard settings, and that
after the Dashboard step the number would appear on the receipt "and nowhere
else". It appears nowhere at all.

## The method failure, which is the part worth keeping

**This was marked CLOSED on the configuration change, nine hours before the
receipt existed — by an entry whose own text said the only proof was a real
receipt.** A configuration change is not evidence of its own effect. The same
shape as the health-check probe recorded the same day: a verification step that
was never actually run against the thing it claims about.

**A gate existed and was one field short**, which is a more useful lesson than
the one this ticket first wrote down. The first draft said *"nothing in the
suite asserts what `lineItems()` produces"*. **That was false, and plan review
caught it.** `verify-stripe-webhook.ts:127-152` already drives `lineItems`
through the injected gateway and pins the count, every amount, the ordering,
the quantity and the currency — seven assertions.

What it has never pinned is `product_data.name`. **So the money on the receipt
was covered and the document was not**: every figure was checked and the words
around them were invisible to the suite. That is the gap, and it is narrower and
sharper than "there was no gate".

## Severity

**High, and live.** A GST registrant must show the registration number on a
receipt for a supply over $30 so the customer can claim an input tax credit.
Every live receipt from 2026-08-20 onward lacks it. Not a money defect — the
tax was charged and collected correctly — but a record defect on the document
that proves it.

## Scope

- The GST registration number reaches **the Stripe receipt**, which is the
  document Stripe issues and the customer keeps.
- It reaches **our approval email** and **our paid confirmation**, both of which
  already itemise the GST amount and are ours to control.
- It does **not** appear where nothing has been charged: the request email, the
  cancellation, the restore.
- The number becomes assertable, and the Stripe line items become asserted at
  all for the first time.

## Risk areas for the reviewer

- **The receipt renders line item NAMES.** Booking #36's receipt showed
  `On-site restoration assessment × 1` and `GST (5%) × 1` — names only, no
  descriptions. So the number must go in the name to be certain it renders;
  putting it in `description` is a guess this ticket cannot test without another
  live charge.
- **An unset value must not print.** `GST (5%) — Reg. undefined` on a tax
  document is worse than the current omission.
- **The cancellation and restore must stay amount-free** — BK-45 asserts they
  carry no fee terms and no `$` figure, and a registration number is a
  tax-document claim that belongs only where money was taken.

## Verification it owes

- The Stripe line items, asserted for the first time, through the gateway params
  `verify-stripe-webhook.ts` already captures — name, amount and count.
- The number present on the approval email and the paid confirmation, absent on
  the request message and on both boundary messages.
- A red row per failure mode, including the unset case.

## Plan

### T1 — the number is a CONSTANT, not an env var

`booking-config.ts` gains `GST_REGISTRATION_NUMBER = '775654577RT0001'`.

**This overturns P9's "built against a `GST_REGISTRATION_NUMBER` env var", and
the reason is the defect this ticket exists to fix.** P9 chose an env var when
the number was unknown and a placeholder was needed. The number is now known,
and it is not a secret — it is printed on every receipt and invoice the business
issues, which is the whole point of it.

An env var here buys nothing and adds one failure mode: **unset in Vercel means
silently absent from every receipt**, which is exactly the state we are in
today and exactly the state nobody noticed for a day. A constant cannot be
forgotten in an environment, and a compliance value that can vanish through
configuration is the wrong shape.

`GST_RATE`-style derivation is untouched — the 5% arithmetic already lives in
`booking-pricing.ts` and this is only the registrant's number.

### T2 — the Stripe line item carries it

```ts
line('GST (5%)', 'Goods and services tax', request.gstCents)
```
becomes a name of the shape `GST (5%) — Reg. 775654577RT0001`.

**In the NAME, evidenced rather than assumed:** booking #36's receipt rendered
`GST (5%) × 1` with no description beside it, so the name is the field known to
survive onto the receipt. The description stays as it is.

### T3 — our two money emails carry it

`approvalMessage`'s Amount block and `customerConfirmation`'s settled block both
already itemise base / travel / GST / total. Each gains one line beneath the
table naming the registrant. Both are the documents a customer keeps, and both
are entirely ours — no Stripe behaviour to guess at.

**Not the request message** (nothing charged), **not the cancellation or the
restore** (no money statement at all, and BK-45 pins their emptiness).

### T4 — the pins, and the gap they close

**P1 — the Stripe line items, asserted for the first time.** Driven through the
injected gateway's captured `params.line_items`: three items with a travel fee
and two without, the right amounts, and the GST name carrying the registration
number. **This is the assertion whose absence let the receipt ship unchecked**,
so it asserts the whole shape rather than only the new part.

**P2** — the approval email and the paid confirmation carry the number; the
request message and both boundary messages do not.

**P3** — the number is one constant with one spelling, asserted to appear in
the emails and the line item as the same string rather than as three literals.

### Assumptions log

- **A1** — a constant, not an env var (T1). Overturns a P9 decision, for the
  reason stated. Overturnable, but the failure mode it removes is the one that
  produced this ticket.
- **A2** — the number goes in the Stripe line item NAME because that is the
  field observed to render on a real receipt. If a future receipt shows
  descriptions too, the name is still correct and no change is owed.
- **A3** — this ticket does not adopt Stripe Tax. That would make Stripe compute
  and label the tax itself and is a far larger change; the client's decision was
  a GST line item priced by us, and P9 locked it.
- **A4 — NOT THIS TICKET'S, raised by the same receipt and recorded:** the
  receipt's contact details are `yegrestoration@gmail.com` and
  `+1 780-720-8856`, while every other surface says `info@yegrestoration.ca`
  and `(780) 479-3285`. That is a Stripe Dashboard setting, not code. Recorded
  in ROADMAP Known traps.

## Plan review (2026-08-20, fresh agent) — three blockers, all real

Each was re-verified before being acted on, including the check digit, which I
computed independently.

### B1 — the ticket's own motivating claim was false · **FIXED**

*"Nothing in the suite asserts what `lineItems()` produces."* It does:
`verify-stripe-webhook.ts:127-152` pins the count, the amounts, the ordering,
the quantity and the currency across two fixtures. **Only `product_data.name`
was never asserted.** Corrected in the problem statement above, and it changes
the work: P1 **extends that block** instead of standing up a parallel arm that
would duplicate seven assertions and give the next reader two places to edit.

### B2 — the "unset value must not print" red row is unreachable, and it is my own rule-rewrite trap · **FIXED**

The risk area and its promised red row were derived under the ENV VAR rule.
**A1 replaced that rule with a constant and the row was carried forward instead
of re-derived** — CLAUDE.md's fourth trap, in the plan that cites it. A
`string` literal has no unset case; manufacturing an `if (!GST_...)` branch
purely to break would be dead code written to be pinned.

Replaced with the failure mode a constant actually has: **a mistyped number**,
which on a tax document is worse than an absent one. The constant is pinned to
CRA's account format `/^\d{9}RT\d{4}$/` **and** to the Business Number's Luhn
check digit — `775654577` sums to 50, valid. A transposition or a single wrong
digit breaks it, which is a real production-target break and a real red row.

### B3 — the number would have printed on a $0 goodwill confirmation · **FIXED**

`customerConfirmation` has **four** money states, not two
(`booking-email.ts:403-405`), and the ticket's absence list named only the
request message and the two boundary messages. A line gated on `!isRequest` or
on `settled` would print a **GST registration number on a document where no GST
was charged** — the `approveFree` visit, and a confirmed row whose snapshot is
missing.

**And the existing $0 guard would not have caught it.** That pin asserts
`!/\$[\d,]+\.\d{2}/` — no computed amount — and `775654577RT0001` contains
no `$` and no decimals, so it would have stayed green with the number printed.
Same shape as the defect that arm was written for.

Gated on **`paidAmounts`**, the same expression the GST *amount* row already
uses, with all four negatives pinned.

### Should-fixes and nits — all adopted

**S4** the idempotency trace (safe, and why) into the Assumptions log, including
that **sessions minted before this deploy keep the old name until they expire**;
**S5** assert on the message captured coming out of the payment route, not only

### Revised A1 — the reasoning the repo already contains

The first draft argued the constant from *"an env var buys nothing"*. The repo
argues it better and had already made this exact decision:

- `booking-config.ts:5-6` — *"Nothing in here reads the environment."* A
  `readEnv` there breaks a stated module invariant, not a preference.
- `booking-config.ts:444-455`, `INTERAC_EMAIL` — the same class of value,
  decided the same way, in the same file: *"a constant rather than an env var

- Every real `readEnv` site is a credential or a runtime toggle.

And both counter-cases fail: there is no legal reason to keep it out of the repo

## Implementation notes

- **The number is asserted, not just rendered.** A constant has no unset case,
  so the failure mode it does have is a typo — and a WRONG registration number
  on a tax document is worse than an absent one, because the customer's
  accountant will act on it. The pin checks CRA's account format and the
  Business Number's Luhn check digit. Red row 1 transposes two digits: the sum
  moves 50 → 48 and the arm fires.
- **`GST_REGISTRATION_LINE` exists so the two surfaces cannot drift.** The
  Stripe line item uses the compact `GST (5%) — Reg. …` because a receipt
  column is narrow; the emails use CRA's spelled-out term because they have
  room and "Reg." is jargon without a receipt's surrounding context. Both
  derive from the one constant, and the pins assert the constant rather than a
  retyped literal — two spellings of a tax number is how one of them ends up
  wrong.
- **The gate on the confirmation is `paidAmounts`, and that is the whole of
  blocker 3.** `!isRequest` or `settled` would have printed the registrant on a
  $0 goodwill visit and on a confirmed row with no snapshot. Red row 3 is
  exactly that loosening, and it fires on the $0 arm in both parts.
- **The existing $0 guard could not have caught it**, which is why the negative
  is asserted directly: that arm tests `!/\$[\d,]+\.\d{2}/`, and
  `775654577RT0001` has no `$` and no decimals.
- **The line-item NAME pins went into the existing block**, per the reviewer's
  B1. That block already had seven money assertions and was green throughout the
  defect; adding a parallel arm would have duplicated them and left two places
  to edit.

## Assumptions log — completed

- **A1 — a CONSTANT, not an env var**, overturning P9. Reasoning restated from
  the house pattern: `booking-config.ts`'s header forbids env reads in that
  module, `INTERAC_EMAIL` is the identical decision in the same file, the number
  is public by design and was already committed in `ROADMAP.md`, and an env var
  unset in preview reproduces precisely the silent absence this ticket fixes.
- **A2 — the line item NAME**, because booking #36's receipt rendered names and
  no descriptions. The SDK documents `description` as *"for your own rendering
  purposes"*, which is not a promise it appears anywhere.
- **A2b — `unit_label` was rejected knowingly.** It is the ONLY product field
  Stripe documents as included in receipts, invoices, Checkout and the customer
  portal — so the next reader will find it and ask. It is still wrong: it labels
  the unit of the quantity, so it would render as a noun beside `× 1` rather
  than as a document-level tax statement.
- **A3 — no Stripe Tax.** It would make Stripe compute and label the tax, which
  contradicts the client's locked decision that GST is a line item priced by us.
- **A4 — idempotency is safe, and here is the trace.**
  `checkoutIdempotencyKey` is `appointment-<id>-approval-<epoch seconds of
  approved_at>`, and the approve route writes `approved_at` unconditionally on
  every approval — so **no re-approval can replay a pre-deploy session under the
  old line-item name.** The only same-key window is two approvals of one
  appointment inside one second, which would raise `StripeIdempotencyError`,
  leave `session` null, and degrade the approval to Interac; pre-existing and
  not worsened here.
  **But sessions minted BEFORE this deploy stay live at Stripe with the old
  name until they expire**, and any receipt issued from one lacks the number.
  No code can retro-fix them. There is exactly one such row in history — booking
  #36, already refunded and cancelled — so the exposure is closed by accident
  rather than by design. **A future deploy of this kind should expire live
  sessions or wait them out.**
- **A5 — the receipt's contact details are NOT this ticket's.**
  `yegrestoration@gmail.com` and `+1 780-720-8856` come from Stripe's business
  settings and no code can change them. Recorded in ROADMAP Known traps.

### Should-fixes — all five fixed

- **S2** — *"a transposition is detectable"* over-claimed, in three places. I

- **S3** — `prepay-flow-spec.md:944` still said the number was pending, in the

- **S4** — red row 4 logged `1` for a break the reviewer measured at `2`. It had

- **S5** — *"Owner: needs a ticket"* sat above the SUPERSEDED marker, reading as
  current. BK-48 is the ticket.

### Nits — N1, N3 and N6 fixed; N2, N4, N5 recorded

**N2 is worth keeping**: nothing pins the literal value, so `775654577RT0001`
could be replaced by any other Luhn-valid number and stay green. The account
portion has no check digit, so no algorithm can close that — it is a
transcription that was checked by eye against the Stripe dashboard, and the pin
guards a typist's slip rather than proving correctness. Stated so the next
reader knows exactly what the green means.

**N4**: the gate is `totalCents > 0`, not `gstCents > 0`. A subtotal of 1–9¢
would round GST to zero and print `$0.00` beside the registrant. Unreachable —
the cheapest tier is $399 — and recorded rather than guarded.
