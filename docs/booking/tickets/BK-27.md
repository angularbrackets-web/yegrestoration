## Why

Client decision, 2026-08-13 (via the user): new conditions for booking an
assessment —

- **Free when the customer proceeds with us.**
- Otherwise: **$699** for a cause-of-loss report and estimate, or **up to
  $1,200** for drafting a sketch/diagram for insurance purposes, including the
  loss report.

User decisions (same day): the terms appear on the **homepage booking section
AND on `/book/`**, with a **required "I understand the assessment terms"
acknowledgment checkbox** before submitting, **recorded in the DB** and
**echoed in the confirmation email**. Admin entry is exempt (same philosophy as
BK-22 — the office books over the phone and explains terms verbally).

Same filtering intent as P7: a visitor who has read "not free if you walk away"
and ticked a box is a better-qualified lead.

## Design decisions

- **Wire/error field name `terms_ack`** (boolean, mirrors `sms_consent`).
  **DB column `terms_acked_at TIMESTAMPTZ`** — the timestamp *is* the
  acknowledgment; no boolean column (consistent with the Locked "no
  `sms_consent` boolean" rule). 422 error field: `terms_ack`. `FIELD_STEPS`
  entry: `terms_ack: 3`.
- **Copy lives in `src/lib/booking-copy.ts`** (the `HAVE_READY_ITEMS` /
  `CANCEL_LINE` home): env-free, importable by the island, `.astro` files, the
  email builder, and `tsx` verify scripts alike. Exports: `FEE_TERMS_HEADING`,
  `FEE_TERMS_INTRO` ("The assessment is free when you proceed with us.
  Otherwise:"), `FEE_TERMS_ITEMS: readonly string[]` (the $699 and
  up-to-$1,200 lines), `FEE_TERMS_ACK_LABEL`. **All wording is placeholder
  pending client sign-off** — this is customer-visible *pricing* language, so
  it goes past the client **before** launch, not after (stricter than the
  standing "client edits later" rule; same carve-out BK-26's checklist has).
- **The checkbox lives on step 3**, directly under a rendered terms box,
  beside the existing SMS-consent checkbox. The acknowledgment must be
  *adjacent* to the terms it acknowledges. Unlike SMS consent, this one
  **blocks** submission.
- `termsAck: boolean` joins `FormValues` (like `smsConsent`), not a new
  `validateStep` parameter — BK-22's `attachmentCount` was a parameter only
  because attachments live outside `FormValues`.
- **Admin exemption rides the existing `entry: 'public' | 'admin'`
  discriminator** in `parseBookingPayload` — the BK-22 seam. `parseAdminEntry`
  needs **no change**. `insertBooking` writes `terms_acked_at` from the shared
  path, so admin rows get NULL with no second statement.

## Client pricing model — FINAL, 2026-08-14 (supersedes everything below)

The client revised the model twice on 2026-08-14. **This is the settled
version**; the section after it is the earlier round, kept because the reasoning
is still useful and because the figures moved twice and the history is the only
protection against putting an old number back.

**The model.** Every customer pays for the assessment at the end of the visit.
If they go ahead with the restoration work, the amount paid is credited in full
against their final invoice — so it costs nothing *in the end* for customers who
proceed. The client's stated reason is that customers "cannot misuse the client
and waste their time". Three tiers, chosen **at booking**:

| Tier | Price | What it buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assessment | **$399 + GST** | The on-site assessment. No documents. |
| + documents | **$699 + GST** | Adds a written cause-of-loss report and repair estimate |
| + insurance sketch | **$1,199 + GST** | Adds a sketch/diagram for an insurance claim; **includes** the report and estimate |

Settled with the client (2026-08-14, relayed by the user):

- **Everyone pays; the credit is what makes it free**, not a waiver. All three
  tiers are credited in full if the customer proceeds.
- **The final invoice will never be less than the assessment fee**, so the
  "credit exceeds the bill" case cannot arise. No refund logic needed.
- **Non-refundable** if the customer does not proceed — "the assessment fees are
  just for the assessment". This must be *stated* at the point of
  acknowledgment; a non-refundable term that is not disclosed is the one most
  likely to be disputed.
- **No time limit on the credit** (client's explicit choice). Recorded as an
  open-ended liability that is immaterial at current volume and revisitable.
- **Payment is on site** — cash, Interac/e-transfer, card-on-site coming. The
  website never takes money; **nothing is charged at booking time.**
- **Tier is chosen at booking, and it is not binding.** Customers may upgrade on
  the day and pay the balance; the upgrade happens outside the system and the
  office records it. So the picker is an expectation-setter, not a commitment,
  and the form must say so or it implies a contract nobody intends.
- **Recording payments is NOT part of this phase** — it belongs to the
  quote → work order → invoice workflow discussion
  (`docs/booking/workflow-options.md`).

**Insurance route — recommendation given to the user 2026-08-14, not yet
client-confirmed.** One rule for everyone: the customer pays the fee themselves
regardless of route and it is credited to their invoice. Do not attempt to bill
the assessment to the insurer — carriers pay for mitigation and repair, not for
a contractor's estimate, which is exactly why most restoration firms give
estimates away on insurance work. On a claim the credit must come off **the
customer's own share** (their deductible portion), or they never see the money
back. **Framing matters and is not cosmetic:** say "your assessment fee is
credited to your invoice", never "we help with your deductible" — contractors
offering to cover deductibles is a known red flag and carriers read it as
rebating. Worth the client running past their broker.

**Replacement copy as drafted — the user gave the go-ahead 2026-08-14 and it is
now implemented, with the departures listed under "Copy revision" below:**

> **Assessment terms**
> The assessment is **$399 + GST**, payable at the end of the visit. If you go
> ahead with the restoration work, we credit the full amount against your final
> invoice.
> • **$399 + GST** — the on-site assessment
> • **$699 + GST** — assessment plus a written cause-of-loss report and repair estimate
> • **$1,199 + GST** — adds a sketch or diagram you can use for an insurance claim (includes the report and estimate)
> Whichever you choose, the full amount comes off your invoice if you hire us.
> The fee is not refundable if you decide not to go ahead.

**"Free Assessment" must now come out site-wide, and this is the part with real
exposure.** Under the old model the claim was conditionally true; under this one
*nobody* gets a free assessment at the point of sale. Canada's Competition
Bureau requires that ads using "free" **"clearly disclose all conditions"**, and
the test weighs *"both the literal meaning of the information and the general
impression it makes"* — a page whose title, nav and CTA all say "Book a Free
Assessment" fails the general-impression half however good the box below is.
That sweep is **BK-29**, and it is a hard blocker on this ticket's deploy, not a
follow-up: the two contradict each other on the same screen.

**Still open with the client:**

1. **Charge for a wasted trip?** (nobody home, no access, cancelled on arrival).
   Industry norm for contractors is free above 48h, 25% at 24–48h, 50%
   same-day/no-show; a *trip charge* is by definition flat and NOT credited,
   unlike an assessment fee. **Recommendation given: publish the expectation,
   charge nothing at launch, revisit once card-on-site is live** — there is no
   card on file, an uncollectable published fee leaves you either chasing $200
   or visibly not enforcing your own terms, and that line would sit in the same
   box as the $399. The $399 already filters at the booking stage, which is
   where the waste was.
2. Insurance-route confirmation (above).
3. Whether the acknowledgment should record **which tier** was chosen —
   recommended yes, lands with BK-31.
4. Whether a $1,199 sketch needs a **longer appointment slot** — assumed no.

### The four constants became five

`FEE_TERMS_OUTRO: readonly string[]` is new, and it is the one structural change.
The ticket said "only the four copy constants change"; that was written before
the drafted copy existed, and the drafted copy has two sentences that cannot go
anywhere else. `FEE_TERMS_INTRO` runs before the tier list and `FEE_TERMS_ITEMS`
is the list — but "whichever you choose…" and "not refundable…" only make sense
*after* the prices. Folding them into the intro would have put "whichever you
choose" before the things being chosen between.

It carries the two disclosures with the most exposure attached:

- **"Nothing is charged when you book."** The site never takes money (locked).
  Without it, a visitor ticking a box beside "$1,199" has every reason to think
  the next button charges them.
- **"It is not refundable if you decide not to go ahead."** The client was
  explicit, and the ticket's own note says an undisclosed non-refundable term is
  the one most likely to be disputed. It renders inside the same box as the
  checkbox on every surface, which is the only thing that makes the
  acknowledgment mean anything.

Rendered on all five surfaces the other constants reach: `/book/`, the homepage
CTA card, the island's step 3, and the confirmation email's HTML **and** text.

### One departure from the drafted wording, and it is a correctness fix

The draft said **"Whichever you choose"** in a context that implied a choice at
booking. **There is no tier picker on the form** — that is BK-31. Shipping the
draft verbatim would have been a claim about a control that does not exist, on
the same screen as three prices.

What shipped instead: *"Tell the tech on the day which of these you want — you
can move up to a higher one then."* True today, true after BK-31 lands, and it
carries the client's own "the tier is not binding" point. The second outro
sentence keeps "whichever you choose", where it reads as "whichever tier applies"
rather than as a control on the page. **BK-31 owns revisiting the first sentence**
when the choice actually moves to booking time.

### "Free Assessment" on the two owned surfaces — fixed here, not parked

Q9 parked the CTA button and the `<h2>` as copy judgment, on the reading that
"free" held for customers who go ahead. **The credit model falsified that
reading**: nobody gets a free assessment at the point of sale. Both strings sat
in the same card as the box quoting $399, which is blocker B1's exact shape.

Reworded to "Book Your Assessment" on both. This is not BK-29 creeping in —
BK-29 owns the ~50 claims on *other* pages; a contradiction inside the priced

**`book.astro`'s `<title>` and WebPage schema `name` still say "Book a Free

### The red pass caught a defect in one of my own new assertions

The credit check was first written as `/credit/i` over the terms block. Its red
pass broke it by rewording the intro to *"the full amount comes off your final
invoice"* — which states the promise perfectly well — **and the gate went red.**
That is a check on a word wearing the costume of a check on a term. It fails
closed, so it was never dangerous, but a gate that reddens on correct copy is a
gate somebody edits out.

re-red by deleting the promise from both sentences rather than rewording it.

This is S1's lesson arriving from the other direction. S1 was an assertion that
stayed green when its claim was broken; this was one that went red when its claim
was kept. Both come from writing the assertion against the sentence in front of
you instead of against the thing you mean.

## Re-review (2026-08-15, fresh agent) — copy revision

Briefed with the ticket, an isolated revision-only delta (the reviewed tree at
HEAD reconstructed and diffed against the working tree), the full working diff,
and gate output. **Verdict: 0 blockers / 4 should-fixes / 3 nits. All seven
folded in. Nothing refused, nothing disputed.**

Three of the four should-fixes were established by **planting the violation and
watching the gates stay green**, not by argument — which is what makes them
worth the space here.

**S-A — the island's terms box had no pin at all. Fixed.** The two `.astro`
surfaces were pinned and the email covered two more behaviourally, which left
`BookingForm.svelte` — *the only surface carrying the checkbox* — covered by

**S-B — `stripForUse` was eating the template after every `https://`. Fixed, and
this was the sharpest finding.** `/\/\/[^\n]*/` is right for frontmatter, where

into `dist/` on the homepage CTA card. **That is red row (n)'s exact violation,
shipping green.** It was already truncating the six `xmlns="http://…"` lines in
that file, and it silently weakened the pre-existing "no obligation" pin too.
Now `/(^|[^:])\/\/[^\n]*/`. The secondary half — `Free&nbsp;Assessment` slipping
a `\s`-based regex — is fixed by normalising entities before the prose pins read.

**S-C — both new claim-level checks were polarity-blind. Fixed, and it is the
same mistake twice.** `/refund/i` is satisfied by *"It is **fully refundable**"* —
the exact negation of the client's decision. The reviewer wrote that sentence,
deleted "Nothing is charged when you book" at the same time, and both gates
stayed green. My red row (u) had only ever *deleted* the word, which proves the
weaker "something mentioning refunds exists" property while reading like the
stronger one — the identical defect the first review found in the `showForm`
pin. Now each check names the accepted phrasings of the *correct* claim, and the
credit check additionally refuses a negated one. The reviewer also noted that
only two of the four material disclosures had assertions at all; **payment
timing and "nothing is charged at booking" now have their own**, which closes
the gap where deleting Q8's answer was green on every gate.

**S-D — the pin's comment claimed something the pin does not deliver. Fixed.**
It said "no visitor reads 'free assessment' on the same screen as the price".
False: the pin reads file text, not the rendered tree, and `book.astro` mounts
`<Navbar />`, whose pill and drawer both say it — `dist/client/book/index.html`

BK-29 gates the deploy and owns `Navbar.svelte`, so this was a wrong claim rather
